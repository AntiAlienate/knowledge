## Why this case matters

This is the first decision of the German Federal Constitutional Court to address, on the merits, the evidentiary status of Parental Alienation Syndrome (PAS) in family-court practice. In a unanimous chamber ruling the Court held that an Oberlandesgericht's reliance on *Eltern-Kind-Entfremdung* (parent-child alienation) by reference to PAS could not, of itself, satisfy Art. 6 Abs. 2 Satz 1 GG, because the PAS construct is "*überkommen*" (outmoded) and "*fachwissenschaftlich als widerlegt geltend[]*" (scientifically refuted). It now stands alongside *Re Y* [2026] EWFC 38 (England & Wales), *NF v AF* [2025] CSOH 13 (Scotland), the Brazilian CFP Nota Técnica 4/2022 and the GREVIO 2022 Germany report as a load-bearing European authority for restraining unreliable alienation evidence in custody and contact litigation.

## Procedural history

The case is a *Verfassungsbeschwerde* (constitutional complaint) under Art. 93 Abs. 1 Nr. 4a GG / § 90 ff. BVerfGG, brought by the mother of two children born in 2012 and 2016 (anonymised by the Court in line with German privacy practice — referred to here simply as **Kind 1** and **Kind 2**). She was represented by **Rechtsanwältin Kerstin Bielefeld**, Hohenzollernring 25, Köln (Rn. front sheet). The complaint was directed against:

- **(a)** the Beschluss of the **Oberlandesgericht Köln** of **17 May 2023** — II-25 UF 19/23 — dismissing her *Anhörungsrüge* (complaint of denied hearing); and
- **(b)** the Beschluss of the **Oberlandesgericht Köln** of **8 May 2023** — II-25 UF 19/23 — transferring the *Aufenthaltsbestimmungsrecht* (right to determine the children's residence) for both children to the father alone under § 1671 Abs. 1 Satz 2 Nr. 2 BGB by way of *einstweilige Anordnung* (preliminary injunction) (Rn. 11).

The complainant invoked **Art. 6 Abs. 2 Satz 1 GG** (Elternrecht / parental right) and **Art. 103 Abs. 1 GG** (Anspruch auf rechtliches Gehör / right to be heard) (Rn. 14). The Court had already granted *einstweilige Anordnung* relief on 15 June 2023 suspending the OLG decision until the principal ruling, on the basis of a *Folgenabwägung* (consequences-balancing) test (Rn. 16). The *Land Nordrhein-Westfalen* and the *Jugendamt* (youth welfare office) declined to make submissions; the father argued the complaint was unfounded; the *Verfahrensbeistand* (guardian ad litem) made no substantive position on the constitutional complaint (Rn. 17).

The chamber accepted the complaint for decision under § 93a Abs. 2 Buchstabe b BVerfGG as "*offensichtlich begründet*" (obviously well-founded) on the Art. 6 Abs. 2 Satz 1 GG ground; the Art. 103 Abs. 1 GG ground was not sufficiently pleaded under § 23 Abs. 1 Satz 2, § 92 BVerfGG (Rn. 18–19). The Court **annulled** the OLG Köln decision of 8 May 2023, declared the Anhörungsrüge decision of 17 May 2023 *gegenstandslos* (moot), and remanded the matter to the Oberlandesgericht (Tenor 1–2; Rn. 35). Costs in both the constitutional-complaint proceeding and the preliminary-injunction proceeding were imposed on the *Land Nordrhein-Westfalen* (Tenor 3; Rn. 36). The decision is *unanfechtbar* (not appealable) (Rn. 37).

## Underlying family proceedings

The parents separated in February 2020. The Court records the relationship as "*hochstrittig*" (highly contentious) and shaped by a multiplicity of child-law proceedings (Rn. 2). The father alleged the mother suffered psychological problems and was alienating the children from him; the mother in turn alleged drug abuse and past violence by the father (Rn. 2).

A first emergency order in June 2020 had transferred the *Aufenthaltsbestimmungsrecht* to the father on the basis that the children had expressed a wish for equal time with both parents and the father undertook to maintain a shared-care model; that order was enforced with the assistance of the *Jugendamt*, a *Gerichtsvollzieher* (court bailiff) and the police, after two earlier police-attended attempts had failed (Rn. 3).

A **Sachverständigengutachten** (court-appointed expert report) obtained in the main custody proceeding in late October 2020 had concluded that the children should have their main residence with the mother on the basis of stronger attachment, recommended generous contact for the father, and considered a 50/50 split inappropriate at the prevailing level of parental conflict (Rn. 4). By Beschluss of 17 February 2021 the *Familiengericht* (Family Court — the *Amtsgericht* sitting as family court) accordingly transferred the *Aufenthaltsbestimmungsrecht* to the mother with near-parity contact for the father; both parents' appeals to the OLG were unsuccessful (Rn. 5).

From at least 30 September 2022 the mother refused to permit further contact between father and children, citing the children's own stated wishes (Rn. 6). Six *Ordnungsmittelbeschlüsse* (coercive-measures orders) against her followed without producing contact (Rn. 6).

On the father's application, a new preliminary-injunction custody proceeding was begun, in which the *Familiengericht* on 15 November 2022 transferred the *Aufenthaltsbestimmungsrecht* to the father without oral hearing on grounds of urgency, and on 14 December 2022 ordered the children's handover, which was enforced on 22 December 2022 by *Gerichtsvollzieher*, police and *Jugendamt* — against the mother's will (Rn. 8). A newly appointed *Verfahrensbeistand* (children's guardian ad litem) thereafter recommended re-transferring residence to the mother, on the basis that the children's clearly expressed wish — even after the dramatic circumstances of removal — was an expression of their *Bindungspräferenzen* (attachment preferences) (Rn. 9). After § 54 Abs. 2 FamFG oral discussion, the Familiengericht lifted its 15 November 2022 order; residence again lay with the mother (Rn. 9).

The father appealed. By the impugned Beschluss of **8 May 2023**, the OLG Köln transferred sole *Aufenthaltsbestimmungsrecht* for both children to the father under § 1671 Abs. 1 Satz 2 Nr. 2 BGB, reasoning that the previously feared *Entfremdung* (alienation) had now materialised, that the mother had ignored multiple court orders, and that — to counteract "*weiterer beziehungsweise vollständiger Entfremdung*" (further or complete alienation) — the residence right had to move to the father even on the assumption that the children rejected contact with him (Rn. 11).

## The Court's reasoning (verbatim with translation)

The Court reaffirmed the *zurückgenommener Prüfungsmaßstab* (reduced standard of constitutional review) applicable outside Art. 6 Abs. 3 GG cases (Rn. 22–23, 27), but emphasised that even under that reduced standard the family court must visibly weigh expert and *Verfahrensbeistand* positions and explain departures from them (Rn. 27, 30).

On the role of the child's wishes (*Kindeswille*), the Court restated (Rn. 24) that nonconformity with the child's expressed will may be justified only "*wenn die Äußerungen des Kindes dessen wirkliche Bindungsverhältnisse, etwa aufgrund Manipulation eines Elternteils, nicht zutreffend bezeichnen*" ("if the child's statements, e.g. on account of manipulation by a parent, do not accurately reflect the child's real attachment relationships") or where following the wish would itself endanger the child's welfare. **Crucially, that conventional formulation must now be read together with Rn. 34, which constrains how a court may go about evidencing such "*Manipulation*".**

On the orientation of the welfare assessment, the Court reiterated (Rn. 26) that "*Maßstab und Ziel einer Sorgerechtsentscheidung ist nicht der Ausgleich persönlicher Defizite zwischen den Eltern, sondern allein das Kindeswohl*" ("the standard and goal of a custody decision is not the resolution of personal deficits between the parents, but solely the welfare of the child").

Applying those principles, the Court found three independent failures in the OLG's reasoning: (i) failure to engage with the prior *Sachverständigengutachten* and *Verfahrensbeistand* position (Rn. 29–30); (ii) inadequate engagement with the clearly expressed wishes of the children (Rn. 31–32), one of whom was nearly twelve; and (iii) impermissible orientation toward sanctioning the mother's conduct rather than toward the child's welfare (Rn. 33).

The load-bearing PAS passage is at **Rn. 34**:

> "Die Entscheidung stellt sich derzeit auch nicht aus anderen Gründen einfachrechtlich als zutreffend dar. Mit der vom Oberlandesgericht herangezogenen Eltern-Kind-Entfremdung wird auf das **überkommene und fachwissenschaftlich als widerlegt geltende Konzept des sogenannten Parental Alienation Syndrom (kurz PAS)** zurückgegriffen. Das genügt als hinreichend tragfähige Grundlage für eine am Kindeswohl orientierte Entscheidung nicht. Soweit ersichtlich besteht nach derzeitigem Stand der Fachwissenschaft kein empirischer Beleg für eine elterliche Manipulation bei kindlicher Ablehnung des anderen Elternteils oder für die Wirksamkeit einer Herausnahme des Kindes aus dem Haushalt des angeblich manipulierenden Elternteils (vgl. umfassend Zimmermann/Fichtner/Walper/Lux/Kindler, in: ZKJ 2023, S. 43 ff., und dies. in: ZKJ 2023, S. 83 ff.)."

(English translation, by the author: "Nor is the decision currently sustainable on other simple-law grounds. The parent-child alienation invoked by the Oberlandesgericht draws on **the outmoded concept — regarded in expert science as refuted — of the so-called Parental Alienation Syndrome (PAS)**. This does not suffice as a sufficiently sound basis for a decision oriented to the welfare of the child. So far as can be seen, under the current state of expert science there is **no empirical evidence of parental manipulation in cases of a child's rejection of the other parent, nor of the effectiveness of removing the child from the household of the allegedly manipulating parent** (see comprehensively Zimmermann/Fichtner/Walper/Lux/Kindler, in: ZKJ 2023, pp. 43 ff., and the same authors in: ZKJ 2023, pp. 83 ff.).")

Two features of this passage warrant emphasis. First, the Court does **not** merely say PAS is contested; it adopts the substantive scientific finding that PAS is "*widerlegt*" (refuted) as a premise of its constitutional analysis. Second, the holding extends beyond the diagnostic label: it expressly denies empirical support both for (a) the **inference** that a child's rejection of one parent evidences manipulation by the other, and (b) the **intervention** of removing the child from the allegedly alienating parent's household. That second prong is a direct constitutional brake on the *Herausnahme*-style remedies that German family courts had inherited from the Anglo-American "reunification" literature.

## What was at issue clinically

The Court did not appoint or evaluate any new clinical evidence; the operative clinical material was the **late-2020 Sachverständigengutachten** in the main proceeding (recommending residence with the mother on attachment grounds) and the **Verfahrensbeistand reports** of the new GAL appointed after the 22 December 2022 enforced handover. Neither expert's identity is given in the published decision and remains **not publicly confirmed**.

Clinically, the OLG had attributed the children's rejection of the father to maternal *Entfremdung*. The BVerfG's holding is that even on the assumption — which the OLG had explicitly made — that the children rejected the father, the inference of maternal manipulation cannot be sustained simply by invoking the *Eltern-Kind-Entfremdung* / PAS construct; and the *Herausnahme* remedy cannot be justified as a clinically effective response, because no empirical evidence supports either limb (Rn. 34). For practitioners, this means a Sachverständige who frames the case as "PAS" or as "einseitig vom Obhutselternteil zu verantwortende Eltern-Kind-Entfremdung" (one-sidedly caretaker-attributable parent-child alienation) is — without more — offering a *nicht hinreichend tragfähige Grundlage* (not sufficiently sound basis) for a welfare-oriented decision, in BVerfG terms.

## Subsequent reception and citation

- **OLG Frankfurt am Main, 7. Familiensenat, Beschluss vom 5 January 2026 — 7 UF 88/25.** The Frankfurt Family Senate held that a Sachverständigengutachten relying on the PAS thesis — or on a one-sidedly caretaker-attributable *Eltern-Kind-Entfremdung* — is *nicht verwertbar* (not usable in evidence), expressly invoking the BVerfG's "pseudowissenschaftlich" characterisation. The court further described the typical PAS circular reasoning ("*Zirkelschluss*") as an *untauglich[e] Grundlage* (unsuitable basis) for a custody decision and made a residence and sole-custody order in favour of the mother in line with the children's expressed wishes. ## Comparative jurisprudence (DE / EW / SCO / BR / WHO–Istanbul)

| Jurisdiction | Authority | Mechanism | Effect on PAS / PA evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germany | **BVerfG 17.11.2023 — 1 BvR 1076/23** (this case) | Constitutional review under Art. 6 Abs. 2 Satz 1 GG; quashes OLG residence-transfer order built on *Eltern-Kind-Entfremdung* | PAS held "überkommen und fachwissenschaftlich als widerlegt"; no empirical basis for either the manipulation inference or the *Herausnahme* remedy (Rn. 34) |
| England & Wales | **Re Y (Children: Alienation – Reopening of Findings)** [2026] EWFC 38 | Family Court of England & Wales sets aside findings made on the basis of evidence by an unregulated expert | Confirms that "alienation" must be evidenced through regulated, methodologically sound clinical opinion; aligns with President's Memorandum on Experts (2024) and *Re C (‘Parental Alienation’; Instruction of Expert)* [2023] EWHC 345 (Fam) |
| Scotland | **NF v AF** [2025] CSOH 13 | Outer House, Court of Session; full proof | Findings of fact of deliberate alienation by a parent — but built on *clinical* expert evidence (Dr MacKinlay, Chartered Clinical Psychologist) not on a PAS-style diagnostic construct; remains the strongest Scottish authority on intentional alienating behaviour without endorsing PAS as a diagnosis |
| India | **A v B** 2026 SCC OnLine Del 276 | Delhi High Court | Treats alienating behaviour as a relevant welfare consideration while declining to import PAS as a diagnostic |
| Brazil | **Conselho Federal de Psicologia, Nota Técnica nº 4/2022** | Regulatory guidance to psychologists | Discourages use of PAS / *Alienação Parental* as a diagnostic construct in forensic reports |
| Istanbul Convention | **GREVIO Baseline Evaluation Report — Germany (2022)**, paras on Art. 31 | Treaty-monitoring report | Criticises German family-court use of "Bindungstoleranz" / alienation framings where they obscure intimate-partner violence |

The BVerfG ruling is the **constitutional-court** addition to a converging European pattern: PAS and PAS-derivative constructs may not, without independent regulated clinical evidence, carry the weight of a welfare decision against an opposing parent.

## What this changes for family-court Sachverständige

For practitioners — particularly *Familiengerichte*, *Verfahrensbeistände*, and *Sachverständige* — the operational consequences are:

1. **A Sachverständigengutachten that frames the case in PAS terms — or in functionally equivalent "*einseitig vom Obhutselternteil zu verantwortende Eltern-Kind-Entfremdung*" terms — is not, by itself, a constitutionally sufficient basis** for a welfare-driven decision (Rn. 34). The Familiengericht must look behind the diagnostic label.
2. **The "*Herausnahme*" remedy** — removing the child from the allegedly alienating parent's household — has now been constitutionally flagged as lacking empirical support for its effectiveness (Rn. 34). Even where alienating behaviour is found, the remedy is not automatic.
3. **The Familiengericht must visibly engage with the prior Sachverständigengutachten and Verfahrensbeistand position** and plausibly explain any departure, even under the reduced standard of review (Rn. 27, 30).
4. **The court must orient toward the child's welfare, not toward sanctioning a parent's conduct** (Rn. 26, 33). This restates BVerfGK 15, 509 <514> but with new bite in the alienation context.
5. **The child's expressed wish, particularly from age ~12, carries non-trivial weight** and may only be set aside on a developed, evidenced basis — not on a generic "alienation" inference (Rn. 24, 31).
6. **A new generation of "*Eltern-Kind-Entfremdung*" expert reports must accordingly establish manipulation through case-specific, methodologically defensible clinical evidence** — not by reasoning from the rejection backwards to its presumed cause. The OLG Frankfurt's reading in *7 UF 88/25* (Jan 2026) — that the PAS-style *Zirkelschluss* is *untauglich* — makes that operational consequence explicit.
