# NF v AF [2025] CSOH 13 — Scotland

**Court:** Outer House, Court of Session (case ref F25/23; remitted from Sheriff Court at Edinburgh)
**Date:** Opinion issued 5 February 2025
**Judge:** The Hon Lord Stuart (designated Family Judge of the Court of Session)
**Reported at:** [Scottish Courts judgment PDF](https://www.scotcourts.gov.uk/media/ba1nsc4o/2025csoh13-nf-against-af.pdf) | [CaseMine commentary](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/uk/emotional-abuse-and-parental-alienation-in-child-welfare:-comprehensive-commentary-on-nf-v-af-2025-csoh-13/view)

## Parties

Anonymised in the judgment. **NF** (the pursuer / father) appeared *party* (i.e. in person), with **Ms Bain** as lay representative. **AF** (the defender / mother) was represented by **Bowan KC** and **Cartwright**, instructed by **MHD Law** solicitors. The court appointed **Ms Shewan** as Curator ad litem to represent the welfare interests of the two children; the curator was independently represented by counsel (**Allison**), instructed by **Millard Law** solicitors (paras [1]-[2] and front sheet).

There are two daughters of the parties' relationship, anonymised by Lord Stuart as "Mary" (born 17 June 2008; aged 16 by the date of opinion, accordingly outside the Court's s.11 jurisdiction) and "Anne" (born 21 September 2011). The parties' relationship began in 2000; they married on 20 September 2014.

## Background

The parties separated on 5 February 2019 when the defender left the family home with the children. On the same day she made a statement to Police Scotland reporting that she had discovered "illicit materials" (the judgment uses this phrase throughout to describe items the court found to be indecent images of children) and that she believed they belonged to the pursuer. In that first statement the defender expressly told police "[the pursuer] has never hit me or the children" and "I have never been concerned about [the pursuer's] interaction, between him and our children" (para [52]).

From 28 February 2019 onwards, however, the defender made escalating allegations: first that the pursuer was a voyeur engaged in "non-recurrent voyeurism", then that the children may have experienced "sexual abuse / inappropriate sexualised behaviour" by the pursuer, then concrete allegations of penetrative sexual abuse of both children (paras [53]-[55]). The children were subjected to two joint investigative interviews each plus a forensic medical examination apiece; none of those investigations produced any supporting evidence and both children "positively stated that they had no memories of any such behaviour" (finding [74](viii)).

The pursuer had no direct contact with the children from 2019 until a single supervised meeting with Anne at a café arranged through the consulting psychologist Dr MacKinlay, which Dr MacKinlay assessed as having gone well but which the defender subsequently reported back as "awkward" and which Anne later reported, after intervening conversations with the defender, as something she "hated" (para [38]).

The case was raised in the Sheriff Court at Edinburgh and remitted to the Court of Session.

## Key facts

- Two daughters, "Mary" (16 by judgment, outside s.11 jurisdiction) and "Anne" (13 at judgment, still subject to s.11 orders)
- Single, sole curator ad litem (Ms Shewan) appointed for the children, separately represented at proof
- Single jointly-consulted expert: **Dr MacKinlay**, **Chartered Clinical Psychologist** ("The family courts are familiar with Dr MacKinlay, and I have no hesitation in confirming that Dr MacKinlay's relevant expertise and experience qualify her to give the skilled opinion evidence she has to assist the court" — para [31]). Three reports: 28 April 2022 (psychological assessment regarding contact), 5 July 2022 (contact between Anne and the pursuer), 8 November 2023 (update)
- Defender's police statement of 5 February 2019: "[the pursuer] has never hit me"
- Defender's escalating statements from 28 February 2019 onwards: voyeurism → sexual abuse of children → penetrative sexual abuse of children
- Two joint investigative interviews and one forensic medical examination per child — none supporting the defender's allegations
- Pursuer sought (a) a residence order for Anne, failing which (b) direct contact with Anne. By the close of submissions he no longer sought direct contact orally
- Defender sought a single specific issue order under s.11(2)(e) Children (Scotland) Act 1995: permission to take Anne out of the UK for up to 21 days per calendar year on holiday without prior consent
- Curator ad litem supported indirect contact only and supported the specific issue order

## Procedural posture

A petition / cause (originating in the Sheriff Court at Edinburgh and remitted to the Court of Session) for s.11 orders under the **Children (Scotland) Act 1995** — residence (s.11(2)(c)), contact (s.11(2)(d)) and specific issue (s.11(2)(e)) orders, with the welfare of the child as the paramount consideration (s.11(7)(a)) and the express requirement that an order may only be made where doing so would be better for the child than making none at all (the "no order" principle). The opinion was issued after a full proof at which the pursuer appeared in person with a lay representative, the defender was represented by senior and junior counsel, and the curator ad litem participated through counsel of her own.

Lord Stuart sets out the statutory framework verbatim at paras [5]-[6], quoting ss.1, 2 and 11 of the 1995 Act, and grounds the analysis in *NJDB v JEG* 2012 SC (UKSC) 293 (Lord Reed) and *White v White* 2001 SC 689 (Lord President Rodger) on the proper judicial approach (paras [7]-[8]). Article 8 ECHR balancing is engaged via the curator's submission (para [9]).

## The judgment

Lord Stuart **refused** the residence order sought by the pursuer. He would also have refused direct contact had it been pressed. He made **no contact order at all** (including no order for indirect contact, despite Dr MacKinlay's and the curator's support for one), and granted only the defender's specific issue order for foreign holidays (paras [69]-[72], [75]).

Crucially, however, the judge made formal **findings of fact** that the defender had emotionally abused both children through false allegations made with the deliberate intention of alienating the pursuer from them, and directed that those findings be communicated to the children through Dr MacKinlay.

The load-bearing language (finding [74](x)):

> "In light of [the] above conclusions, in my judgment, the defender has engaged in a course of conduct where she has knowingly made false and increasingly serious allegations against the pursuer concerning abuse of the children, including sexual abuse, and deliberately misrepresented the children's medical symptoms in support of those false allegations, all with the intention of alienating the pursuer from the children." (Lord Stuart, finding [74](x))

The conduct was characterised in the substantive analysis as direct emotional abuse:

> "Having reached that conclusion, it follows from the unchallenged expert evidence of Dr MacKinlay, which I accept, the defender's actions in telling Mary and Anne that the pursuer had sole possession of the illicit materials and by reporting the pursuer's possession of the illicit materials to the police, which ultimately contributed to the alienation of the pursuer from the children, constitutes direct emotional abuse of Mary and Anne by the defender." (para [51])

And earlier, accepting the curator's framing:

> "I accept that the evidence in this case supports the conclusion that the defender's actions have been abusive of the children and consequently caused them harm, not least by alienating the pursuer from them but by also leading them to believe that they have been abused by the pursuer." (para [69])

Despite those findings, the welfare-paramount and "no order" principles of s.11(7) drove the result. The court accepted Dr MacKinlay's clinical evidence that any historical emotional abuse was now largely past; that the loss of the pursuer was "a past loss" whereas the threatened loss of the defender and elder sister "would be a future loss"; that Anne was happy at home and at school; and that, applying s.11(7)(a) strictly, the court could not "properly conclude that making a residence order in respect of Anne in favour of the pursuer would be better for Anne than that none should be made at all" (para [69]).

Lord Stuart accepted the curator's submission that the children themselves had a right to know the court's findings and ordered that Dr MacKinlay communicate the formal findings to the children directly (paras [73]-[74]).

## Why this case matters

NF v AF is the **strongest explicit finding by an Outer House senator that a parent's course of false allegations against the other parent constitutes "direct emotional abuse" of the children carried out with "the intention of alienating" the other parent from them**. Three things make it significant:

1. **The "alienation" language is judicial, not expert-borrowed.** Lord Stuart does not adopt "parental alienation" as a syndrome or diagnosis. He uses the verb "alienating" and the noun "alienation" as ordinary-language descriptions of *what the defender was doing* — a finding of fact, not a theoretical construct. That sidesteps the long-running Scottish argument over whether "PA" is a recognised concept (the Scottish Government declined to add it to the s.11 welfare checklist; see [Scottish Government FOI 2021-00132015](https://www.gov.scot/publications/foi-202100132015/)) and instead grounds the finding in the existing statutory definition of "abuse" at s.11(7C), which already covers "any other conduct giving rise … to physical or mental injury, fear, alarm or distress." Knowingly fabricating sexual-abuse allegations against the other parent, repeated to the children, is plainly such conduct.

2. **It demonstrates that Scots law does not need a statutory PA concept to produce a substantive PA finding.** The case directly answers Shared Parenting Scotland's argument that English jurisprudence had drifted ahead of Scotland: a Scottish Outer House senator, applying only the 1995 Act and the Lord Reed / Lord President Rodger guidance, has now made the same kind of finding the English Court of Appeal made in *Re S* [2020] EWCA Civ 568 and *Re H-N* [2021] EWCA Civ 448, in the same kind of fact pattern (escalating, evidentially unsupported sexual-abuse allegations operating to sever contact). For practitioners that closes the asymmetry: there is now a named Scottish authority to cite.

3. **The remedial outcome is uncomfortable.** Lord Stuart found alienating emotional abuse on the facts but refused both a residence transfer and any direct contact order, applying the s.11(7) welfare-paramount and "no order" tests strictly on Dr MacKinlay's evidence that change would now harm Anne more than the status quo. The case therefore stands for two compatible propositions in Scots law: (a) findings of alienating conduct can be made on the existing statutory definition of abuse, and (b) those findings will not automatically displace the welfare-paramount calculation against transfer. That is the same Re S-style "least harmful" reasoning the English Court of Appeal has accepted — and it forecloses any reform argument that recognising alienation requires a remedies regime that overrides welfare.

The case also matters in the live Scottish reform debate. **Scottish Petition PE2057** ("Promote shared parenting and prevent the separation of children from their parents") was closed by the Scottish Parliament on 24 January 2024 ([petition page](https://petitions.parliament.scot/petitions/PE2057)) without statutory PA recognition. NF v AF arrived twelve months later and effectively delivers — through judicial fact-finding under existing statute — what the petition was unable to secure through legislation. Whether that judicial route is enough, or whether Scottish Parliament reform is still needed, is now the central question for the recognition camp (Shared Parenting Scotland) and the critique camp (Scottish Women's Aid, Engender) alike. (See the [/practitioners/lawyers/uk-devolved.md](../practitioners/lawyers/uk-devolved.md) Scotland section, entries S2, S7 and S8, for the institutional landscape.)

Compared with the English arc — *Re A (Children) (Parental Alienation)* [2019] EWFC B56 → *Re S* [2020] EWCA Civ 568 → *Re H-N* [2021] EWCA Civ 448 → *Re Y (Experts and Alienating Behaviour: The Modern Approach)* [2026] EWFC 38 — Scotland has skipped the messy expert-credentialling fight that consumed England and arrived at a textually conservative finding rooted in the statutory definition of abuse. The Outer House did this without an unregulated PA expert: Dr MacKinlay is a chartered clinical psychologist whose evidence Lord Stuart describes as "unchallenged", which insulates the finding against the kind of set-aside attack that succeeded in *Re Y*. In that respect NF v AF may turn out to be more durable, and more persuasive in further Scottish proceedings, than the high-profile English cases it parallels.
