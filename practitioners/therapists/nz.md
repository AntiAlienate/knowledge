# New Zealand Therapists & Clinicians — Parental Alienation Practitioner Directory

**Schema version:** 1.0
**Last updated:** 2026-05-25
**License:** CC BY 4.0 (intended for github.com/AntiAlienate/antialienate-knowledge)
**Scope:** New Zealand-based registered psychologists (under the NZ Psychologists Board / Health Practitioners Competence Assurance Act 2003), NZAC-registered counsellors, NZ-registered psychotherapists (PBANZ / APANZ), social workers (SWRB), child & adolescent psychotherapists, and clinically-adjacent academics who have (a) operated or directed PA-specific therapeutic / mediation / reunification interventions in NZ, (b) given expert-witness or s.133 / s.46G evidence in NZ Family Court PA cases, (c) published peer-reviewed work on PA / alienating behaviour / family-violence overlap, or (d) sat on the institutional bodies shaping NZ Family Court therapeutic practice (FVDRC, NZ Psychologists Board, NZCCP, Family Court Specialist Report Writers). Stance is sourced, not inferred.

**Standards:** All entries verified from primary sources (NZ Psychologists Board register at https://register.psychologistsboard.org.nz/, NZAC / NZCCP / APANZ / SWRB listings, practice / institutional websites, NZLII / Courts of NZ judgment portal, peer-reviewed publications, named press). Where a fact could not be confirmed from a public primary source it is flagged "**not publicly confirmed**".

**Stance taxonomy:**
- `recognition` — treats parental alienation / alienating behaviours as a real, assessable phenomenon; operates or endorses PA-specific identification / mediation / reunification work in NZ
- `critique` — argues the PA construct is operationalised against family-violence survivors (especially mothers and children) in the NZ Family Court; advocates DV/trauma-informed alternatives and tighter regulation of s.133 / s.46G report writers
- `middle` — engages both alienating-behaviour and family-violence frames; differential-diagnosis approach aligned with NZ Family Court Specialist Report Writer Practice Note and the post-2019 Te Korowai Ture-a-Whānau "child-centred + safety-primary" line

**Tier:** 1 = primary outreach (centrally cited in the NZ PA debate at academic / institutional / press level); 2 = secondary (relevant clinical or institutional voice); 3 = background / institutional reference.

**Critical NZ regulatory & policy context (must read before using this directory):**
- The **NZ Psychologists Board / Te Poari Kaimātai Hinengaro o Aotearoa** is the regulatory authority appointed under the Health Practitioners Competence Assurance Act 2003 — https://psychologistsboard.org.nz/ . The public register is at https://register.psychologistsboard.org.nz/ . Six scopes of practice (psychologist; clinical psychologist; educational psychologist; counselling psychologist; intern psychologist; trainee psychologist).
- **Counsellors and psychotherapists** are *not* under the National Law. Counsellors self-regulate via **NZAC** (NZ Association of Counsellors, https://www.nzac.org.nz/) and the **NZ Christian Counsellors Association**. Psychotherapists are regulated by the **Psychotherapists Board of Aotearoa New Zealand (PBANZ)** under HPCAA 2003 — https://www.pbanz.org.nz/ — with **APANZ** (Association of Psychotherapists Aotearoa NZ, https://apanz.org.nz/) as the professional body. Social workers are regulated by the **Social Workers Registration Board (SWRB)** — https://swrb.govt.nz/ .
- **Care of Children Act 2004 s.133** authorises the Family Court to order a psychological report on a child by a Court-appointed specialist report writer. Practice Note "Specialist Report Writers" (updated 2018; on Psychologists Board site at https://psychologistsboard.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/FC-Practice-Note-090718.pdf ); the Family Court / Ministry of Justice / NZLS Family Law Section / NZ Psychologists Board / NZ Psychological Society / NZCCP jointly settled the original terms.
- **s.46G Care of Children Act 2004** — counselling after proceedings commenced; the section under which alienation-aware therapy is most commonly ordered. Counselling qualifications are linked to the Ministry of Justice approved-counsellor list which includes clinical psychology.
- There is **no NZ appellate-level equivalent of England's *Re Y* [2026] EWFC 38** restricting unregulated PA experts. Standards-of-evidence concerns are noted at entry level (Backbone Collective, Leonetti 2023 *UCLA Pacific Basin Law Journal*, FVDRC reports).
- The most active NZ recognition-camp practice is **The Family Matters Centre** (Jill Goldson, Auckland) — the Goldson Model has been adopted by the NZ government for child-inclusive out-of-court mediation in the "Bridging the Gap" online programme. There is no NZ-based PA-specific NGO equivalent to Australia's EMMM Foundation; the closest cross-jurisdictional structure is **PAANZ** (Parental Alienation Australia and New Zealand), founded 2012, primarily Australia-based (Korosi, Moriarty).
- The most institutionally-weighted **critique-camp** voice in NZ clinical-academic space is the **Family Violence Death Review Committee (FVDRC)** which has had Denise Wilson and Pauline Gulliver as members (chaired by Julia Tolmie 2011-2016 — see `/practitioners-nz-lawyers.md` entry 7).
