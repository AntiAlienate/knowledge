# Codice Civile art. 316 — Responsabilità genitoriale

> **TL;DR.** Italian Codice Civile article 316 is the **foundational responsabilità genitoriale statute** — the general rule applicable to both married and unmarried parents (regardless of whether the parents have separated). **First paragraph**: both parents hold parental responsibility, exercised by common accord, taking into account the child's capacities, natural inclinations and aspirations; the parents agree the child's habitual residence and choices about education. **Second paragraph**: in case of conflict on particularly important matters (including habitual residence and choice of school), each parent may apply to the court without formalities, indicating proposed solutions. **Third paragraph**: the court, having heard the parents and arranged the hearing of the child (mandatory for children aged 12+, optional for younger children capable of discernment), attempts to reach an agreed solution; failing that, adopts the solution best serving the child's interest. **Fourth paragraph**: applies to unmarried parents — the parent who has acknowledged the child exercises parental responsibility; if both acknowledge, both exercise jointly. **Fifth paragraph**: the parent not exercising parental responsibility nevertheless retains oversight ("vigila") over the child's education, instruction and conditions of life.

## Full statutory text — verbatim Italian

### Art. 316 — Responsabilità genitoriale

**Primo comma.** Entrambi i genitori hanno la responsabilità genitoriale che è esercitata di comune accordo tenendo conto delle capacità, delle inclinazioni naturali e delle aspirazioni del figlio. I genitori di comune accordo stabiliscono la residenza abituale del minore e adottano le scelte relative alla sua istruzione ed educazione.

**Secondo comma.** In caso di contrasto su questioni di particolare importanza, tra le quali quelle relative alla residenza abituale e all'istituto scolastico del figlio minorenne, ciascuno dei genitori può ricorrere senza formalità al giudice indicando i provvedimenti che ritiene più idonei.

**Terzo comma.** Il giudice, sentiti i genitori e disposto l'ascolto del figlio minore che abbia compiuto gli anni dodici e anche di età inferiore ove capace di discernimento, tenta di raggiungere una soluzione concordata e, ove questa non sia possibile, adotta la soluzione che ritiene più adeguata all'interesse del figlio.

**Quarto comma.** Il genitore che ha riconosciuto il figlio esercita la responsabilità genitoriale su di lui. Se il riconoscimento del figlio, nato fuori del matrimonio, è fatto dai genitori, l'esercizio della responsabilità genitoriale spetta ad entrambi.

**Quinto comma.** Il genitore che non esercita la responsabilità genitoriale vigila sull'istruzione, sull'educazione e sulle condizioni di vita del figlio.

## English translation (working — not an official translation)

### Article 316 — Parental Responsibility

**First paragraph.** Both parents hold parental responsibility, which is exercised by common accord, taking account of the capacities, natural inclinations and aspirations of the child. The parents shall by common accord establish the habitual residence of the minor and make decisions relating to the child's instruction and education.

**Second paragraph.** In case of conflict on matters of particular importance — including those relating to the habitual residence and the choice of school of the minor child — each parent may apply without formalities to the court, indicating the measures considered most appropriate.

**Third paragraph.** The court, having heard the parents and arranged the hearing of the minor child who has reached the age of twelve and also of a younger age where capable of discernment, shall attempt to reach an agreed solution; where this is not possible, shall adopt the solution it considers most adequate to the interest of the child.

**Fourth paragraph.** The parent who has acknowledged the child exercises parental responsibility over the child. Where the acknowledgment of the child, born out of marriage, is made by both parents, the exercise of parental responsibility belongs to both.

**Fifth paragraph.** The parent not exercising parental responsibility shall oversee the instruction, education and conditions of life of the child.

## Why article 316 matters for parental-alienation analysis

Article 316 establishes the **foundational substantive framework** within which PA-context conflicts arise. Several provisions are directly relevant:

- **First paragraph — common-accord exercise**: parental responsibility is exercised by common accord. PA-pattern conduct that systematically excludes the other parent from the common-accord framework — e.g., unilateral changes to schooling, religion, residence — directly violates this baseline. The text's reference to "capacities, natural inclinations and aspirations of the child" parallels the discernment-of-coached-versus-authentic-wishes analysis applicable in PA cases.

- **Second paragraph — court access on particular importance**: each parent may apply to the court without formalities on disputes of particular importance (with the canonical examples of habitual residence and choice of school). PA-context disputes — e.g., one parent's unilateral decision to relocate with the child, or to enroll the child in a new school in a manner that disrupts contact — fit squarely within this informal court-access route.

- **Third paragraph — mandatory child hearing at 12+ + child-of-discernment hearing below 12**: the mandatory hearing rule (ascolto del minore) is the canonical PA-analytical hook for assessing whether a child's expressed wishes are autonomously held or coached. Italian Cassazione jurisprudence has developed extensive case-law on the discernment threshold and the techniques for evaluating coaching effects in the child-hearing process.

- **Fourth paragraph — unmarried-parent symmetry**: parental responsibility belongs to both parents who have acknowledged the child. This statutory equality of unmarried-parent rights is the substantive baseline that PA-pattern conduct (especially in unmarried-parent contexts) attempts to disrupt.

- **Fifth paragraph — oversight role for non-exercising parent**: even where one parent does not exercise day-to-day parental responsibility, that parent retains a "vigila" (oversight) role over the child's education, instruction and conditions of life. This is the statutory basis for the non-residential parent's standing to challenge alienating-pattern conduct that affects the child's broader welfare.

Article 316 operates upstream of article 337-ter (which applies in the separation/divorce/dissolution context). PA-context conflicts may arise under article 316 before any separation proceedings have been initiated — e.g., unmarried parents in conflict over schooling — and then transition to the article 337-ter framework upon separation.
