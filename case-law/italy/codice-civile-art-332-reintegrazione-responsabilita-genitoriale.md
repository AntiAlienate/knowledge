# Codice Civile, art. 332 — Reintegrazione nella responsabilità genitoriale

**Jurisdiction:** Italy · **Citation:** Codice Civile, art. 332 · **Authoritative source:** [brocardi.it — art. 332](https://www.brocardi.it/codice-civile/libro-primo/titolo-ix/capo-i/art332.html)

The **rehabilitation/restoration** provision in the Italian graduated parental-responsibility-removal framework. Where a parent has been deprived of *responsabilità genitoriale* under [art. 330](codice-civile-art-330-decadenza-responsabilita-genitoriale.md), art. 332 provides the statutory route back: the court can *reintegrare* (reinstate) the parent when (a) the circumstances that gave rise to the deprivation have ceased and (b) there is no remaining risk of prejudice to the child.

Completes the graduated-remedy triad:
- [art. 333](codice-civile-art-333-condotta-pregiudizievole.md) — intermediate measures for *pregiudizievole* conduct
- [art. 330](codice-civile-art-330-decadenza-responsabilita-genitoriale.md) — *decadenza* for *grave pregiudizio*
- **art. 332** — *reintegrazione* when conditions are restored

## Verbatim text (current consolidated)

> **Art. 332 — Reintegrazione nella responsabilità genitoriale**
>
> Il giudice può reintegrare nella responsabilità genitoriale il genitore che ne è decaduto, quando, cessate le ragioni per le quali la decadenza è stata pronunciata, è escluso ogni pericolo di pregiudizio per il figlio.

## English working translation

> **Art. 332 — Reinstatement in parental responsibility**
>
> The judge may reinstate in parental responsibility the parent who has been deprived of it, when, the reasons for which the deprivation was pronounced having ceased, every risk of prejudice to the child is excluded.

## Why this matters for PA

- **The reciprocal of decadenza.** Art. 330 deprivation is not permanent — art. 332 provides for restoration when circumstances change. This is doctrinally significant: Italian family law explicitly contemplates and structures rehabilitation, not permanent disqualification.
- **Two-prong test.** Reintegration requires both: (a) *cessate le ragioni* (the reasons that gave rise to deprivation have ceased) — a backward-looking finding that the harmful conduct has substantially stopped; and (b) *escluso ogni pericolo di pregiudizio* (every risk of prejudice excluded) — a forward-looking finding that resumption of authority will not pose risk. The Cassazione has held both must be made out; partial cessation is insufficient.
- **Use in PA cases.** Where an alienating parent has had *responsabilità genitoriale* removed under art. 330 and subsequently engages with therapeutic intervention, completes parenting programmes, and demonstrates sustained behavioural change, art. 332 is the route to restoration. The Cassazione's approach is cautious — reintegration typically requires multi-year demonstration of behavioural change and exclusion of any continuing alienating-conduct pattern.
- **Standing.** The parent seeking reintegration has standing to apply directly. *Pubblico Ministero* may also intervene to support or oppose reintegration where the child-welfare interest requires it. The *servizio sociale* (social services) typically provides the welfare assessment underpinning the *cessate le ragioni* and *escluso pericolo* findings.
- **No automatic restoration.** Decadenza is not time-limited; there is no statutory clock that automatically restores. Art. 332 requires affirmative judicial action on application or *ex officio* review. This is a deliberate design — the burden is on the previously-deprived parent to show rehabilitation, not on the system to restore by default.

## Procedural application

- Reintegration proceedings run in the same court that pronounced the *decadenza* (typically the *Tribunale per i Minorenni*; post-Cartabia transition: *Tribunale della Famiglia*).
- Procedure follows the *Codice di Procedura Civile* (post-Cartabia: art. 473-bis et seq.) family-procedure framework. The court typically commissions a *CTU* (court-appointed expert) to assess both prongs of the art. 332 test.
- Where reintegration is granted, it may be on conditions (e.g. continued therapeutic supervision, ongoing monitoring by *servizio sociale*) and remains revocable if conditions change.

## Relationship to art. 330 and 333

- **art. 333** — flexible intermediate measures; revocable at any time.
- **art. 330** — maximum-tier decadenza; requires *grave pregiudizio*; not automatically time-limited.
- **art. 332** — restoration route from art. 330 decadenza; requires both prongs (cessate ragioni + escluso pericolo).
- Together, these three articles establish a coherent rehabilitation-oriented framework: severity of remedy scales with severity of conduct, and restoration is available when underlying conditions have been resolved.

## Use in alienating-conduct rehabilitation

The Cassazione has been cautious in applying art. 332 to PA cases. Sustained behavioural change must be demonstrated:
- Completion of court-ordered therapeutic intervention
- Documented absence of recent alienating conduct (typically multi-year)
- Independent professional assessment supporting the *escluso pericolo* prong
- The child's expressed wishes (where age-appropriate) are heard but not determinative — the analysis is forward-looking welfare assessment, not consent

Where these conditions are met, art. 332 provides the formal restoration route. This is structurally important: even at the highest tier of remedy, the Italian framework retains rehabilitation as the orienting purpose.
