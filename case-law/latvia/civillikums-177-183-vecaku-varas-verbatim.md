# Latvia Civillikums — Aizgādības and Saskarsmes tiesības (Verbatim)

## TL;DR

Latvia's **Civillikums** (Civil Code) is a 1937 code restored in 1993 by post-independence Latvia. Book One (**Gimenes tiesibas** — Family Rights), Chapter 8, arts. 177 – 203 govern parental authority (**aizgadniba**) and the child's contact rights (**saskarsmes tiesibas**). The chapter has been amended several times — most substantively in 2003, 2013 and 2018 — to align with post-Soviet reforms and the EU/ECHR framework. Four provisions are the operational anti-alienation core: **art. 177(1) – (2)** (equal rights of parents; joint exercise of aizgadniba); **art. 178.1** (a parent living apart retains aizgadniba unless a court decides otherwise); **art. 181(1)** (child's right to personal contact with the parent living apart); **art. 182(1)** (residential parent's positive duty to promote contact and prohibition of obstruction). Enforcement runs through the orphan's court (**barintiesa**) at the administrative layer and the civil court under the Civilprocesa likums at the judicial layer. The Latvian text below is the consolidated version in the Likumi.lv publication of Civillikums Book One; the English is a working translation for research use. The Latvian original controls.

## Statute — Verbatim Text

### Art. 177 Civillikums — Aizgādības jēdziens un vecāku vienlīdzība (Concept of parental authority and equality of parents)

**Latvian (consolidated text):**

> (1) Bērns lidz pilngadibas sasniegsanai ir vecaku aizgadibā.
>
> (2) Aizgādība ir vecāku tiesības un pienākums rūpēties par bērnu un viņa mantu un pārstāvēt bērnu viņa personiskajās un mantiskajās attiecībās.
>
> (3) Rūpes par bērnu nozīmē viņa aprūpi, uzraudzību un tiesības noteikt viņa dzīvesvietu.
>
> (4) Bērna aprūpe nozīmē viņa uzturēšanu, tas ir, ēdiena, apģērba, mājokļa un veselības aprūpes nodrošināšanu, bērna kopšanu un viņa izglītošanu un audzināšanu (pienācīgas garīgās un fiziskās attīstības nodrošināšanu, iespēju robežās ievērojot viņa individualitāti, spējas un intereses un sagatavojot bērnu sabiedriski derīgam darbam).

**English (working translation):**

> (1) A child, until the attainment of the age of majority, is in the aizgadniba (parental authority) of the parents.
>
> (2) Aizgadniba is the right and duty of the parents to care for the child and the child's property and to represent the child in personal and property matters.
>
> (3) Care for the child means the child's aprūpe (upbringing), supervision, and the right to determine the child's place of residence.
>
> (4) Aprūpe of the child means providing for the child, that is, ensuring food, clothing, housing and health care, tending to the child, and educating and raising the child (ensuring appropriate spiritual and physical development, respecting to the extent possible the child's individuality, abilities and interests, and preparing the child for socially useful work).

**Operational note:** The statute treats aizgadniba as a *composite* right — care + supervision + representation + residence-fixing. The composite structure matters in alienation litigation because unilateral acts that break one element (e.g. the residential parent making unilateral medical or schooling decisions, or unilaterally relocating residence within Latvia) are breaches of the aizgadniba composite even if physical contact is nominally preserved.

### Art. 178 — Aizgādības kopīga izmantošana (Joint exercise of aizgadniba)

**Latvian:**

> (1) Aizgādības tiesības vecākiem ir vienādas.
>
> (2) Kamēr laulība nav šķirta vai atzīta par neesošu, vecāki aizgādību izlieto kopīgi.
>
> (3) Ja vecāki nespēj vienoties, viņu strīdu izsķir tiesa.

**English:**

> (1) Parents have equal aizgadniba rights.
>
> (2) So long as the marriage has not been dissolved or declared void, the parents exercise aizgadniba jointly.
>
> (3) If the parents cannot reach agreement, their dispute shall be resolved by the court.

**Operational note:** Equality plus joint exercise plus court-referral is the same architecture as Estonia § 116, Lithuania § 3.159 and Malta art. 143A. The Baltic and Malta drafting tradition converges on this exact three-element sequence.

### Art. 178.1 — Aizgādība pēc laulības šķiršanas un vecāku šķirtas dzīves gadījumā (Aizgadniba after divorce and when parents live separately)

**Latvian (consolidated text):**

> (1) Pēc laulības šķiršanas vai vecāku šķirtas dzīves saglabājas kopīga vecāku aizgādība, ja tiesa nav nolemusi citādi.
>
> (2) Kopīga aizgādība izbeidzas, ja vecāki vienojas vai tiesa nolemj par vienam no vecākiem nodibinātu atsevišķu aizgādību.
>
> (3) Atsevišķa aizgādība vienam no vecākiem nodibināma, kad kopīgas aizgādības saglabāšana nav iespējama un tas ir bērna interesēs.

**English:**

> (1) After the dissolution of the marriage or where the parents live separately, joint aizgadniba is preserved, unless the court decides otherwise.
>
> (2) Joint aizgadniba shall terminate where the parents agree, or where the court decides that sole aizgadniba is to be established with one of the parents.
>
> (3) Sole aizgadniba with one of the parents shall be established where the preservation of joint aizgadniba is not possible and this is in the interests of the child.

**Operational note:** Article 178.1(1) is the *statutory presumption of continuation*. The default state on separation is joint aizgadniba; sole aizgadniba requires (i) impossibility of joint aizgadniba AND (ii) an affirmative best-interests finding. The two conjuncts are both required — a finding of "impossibility" alone does not authorise sole aizgadniba. This double-conjunction is the strongest textual barrier to sole-custody drift in the Baltic block.

### Art. 178.2 — Atsevišķas aizgādības apjoms (Scope of sole aizgadniba)

**Latvian:**

> (1) Ja tiesa vienam no vecākiem nodibinājusi atsevišķu aizgādību, otram vecākam ir tiesības uz saskarsmi ar bērnu un tiesības tikt informētam par bērna dzīvi.
>
> (2) Atsevišķa aizgādība neizslēdz otra vecāka pienākumu uzturēt bērnu.

**English:**

> (1) Where the court has established sole aizgadniba with one of the parents, the other parent has the right of saskarsme (contact) with the child and the right to be informed about the child's life.
>
> (2) Sole aizgadniba does not extinguish the other parent's obligation to maintain the child.

**Operational note:** Even at the far end — where sole aizgadniba has been ordered — the non-residential parent retains two statutory rights: contact (paragraph 1) and information (paragraph 1). Latvia treats information as a distinct right, separately actionable from contact. Information starvation is therefore a distinct wrong under Latvian law, not merely evidence of obstruction.

### Art. 179 — Aizgādības tiesību apjoms attiecībās ar trešajām personām (Scope of aizgadniba in dealings with third parties)

**Latvian:**

> Vecākiem viņu aizgādības tiesību ietvaros ir pienākums un tiesības pārstāvēt bērnu viņa personiskajās un mantiskajās attiecībās. Kopīgas aizgādības gadījumā vecāki bērnu pārstāv kopīgi.

**English:**

> Within their aizgadniba, parents have the duty and the right to represent the child in personal and property matters. In the case of joint aizgadniba, the parents represent the child jointly.

**Operational note:** Under joint aizgadniba (art. 178.1(1) presumption), both parents together represent the child in dealings with schools, health services, passport issuance, banking and litigation. The residential parent has no unilateral representation power. In practical alienation-litigation terms, schools and doctors that accept the residential parent's sole instructions are acting outside art. 179 — the "signature line" is joint.

### Art. 181 — Bērna saskarsmes tiesības (Child's contact rights)

**Latvian (consolidated text):**

> (1) Bērnam ir tiesības uztureti personiskas attiecibas un tiesu kontaktus ar jebkuru no vecakiem (saskarsmes tiesibas).
>
> (2) Kaduram vecakam ir pienakums un tiesibas uzturet personiskas attiecibas un tiesus kontaktus ar berrnu. Sis noteikums piemerojams ari tad, ja berns skirts no gimenes vai dzivo skirti no viena vai abiem vecakiem.
>
> (3) Vecakam, kurs nedzivo kopa ar bernu, ir tiesibas sanjemt zinas par bernu, it īpasi tada zinas, kas attiecas uz viņa attistibu, veselibu, macibam, sekmem un sadzives apstakliem.

**English:**

> (1) The child has the right to maintain personal relations and direct contacts with each of the parents (saskarsmes tiesibas — contact rights).
>
> (2) Each parent has the duty and the right to maintain personal relations and direct contacts with the child. This provision applies also where the child is separated from the family or lives apart from one or both parents.
>
> (3) The parent who does not live with the child has the right to receive information about the child, in particular information relating to the child's development, health, education, achievements and living conditions.

**Operational note:** Article 181 is drafted as **a child's right first** (paragraph 1) and a *coextensive* parental right-and-duty (paragraph 2). Paragraph 3 makes the *information* right free-standing — the non-residential parent's right to information exists regardless of whether contact is in fact taking place, and is enforceable independently. In pleading terms:

  - The *child's* right of contact under art. 181(1) is invoked when the child's own participation in the case is relevant (representation by an outside interests-representative, guardian, or barintiesa).
  - The parent's paired right-and-duty under art. 181(2) is invoked in the parent's own name.
  - The information right under art. 181(3) is invoked separately and is often the fastest route to an early injunction in "silent obstruction" cases where the residential parent has cut off updates but is nominally allowing contact.

### Art. 182 — Vecāku pienākums veicināt saskarsmi (Parents' duty to promote contact)

**Latvian:**

> (1) Vecākiem ir pienākums veicināt bērna saskarsmes tiesības ar to vecāku, ar kuru bērns nedzīvo kopā.
>
> (2) Aizliegts izmantot aizgādības tiesības pretēji bērna interesēm.
>
> (3) Ja vecāki nespēj vienoties par saskarsmes tiesību izlietošanu, saskarsmes kārtību nosaka tiesa.

**English:**

> (1) The parents have the duty to promote the child's contact rights with the parent with whom the child does not reside.
>
> (2) It is prohibited to use aizgadniba rights contrary to the interests of the child.
>
> (3) Where the parents cannot agree on the exercise of contact rights, the order of contact shall be fixed by the court.

**Operational note:** Article 182(1) is a **positive duty of promotion**, not a duty of non-obstruction. The residential parent must *actively promote* contact — passive tolerance is not enough. Article 182(2) is the anti-abuse-of-rights clause specific to aizgadniba: exercising aizgadniba to cut off the other parent from the child is *ipso facto* an unlawful use of the right. Article 182(3) is the residual court-order jurisdiction where agreement fails. The trio is the Latvian counterpart of Estonia § 143(2) ("acts which harm the relations") and Lithuania § 3.170(3) (*kliudymas* prohibition); it is textually the most *positive* of the three, requiring not merely non-obstruction but promotion.

### Art. 183 — Vecāku aizgādības tiesību izmantošanas nosacījumi (Conditions on exercise of aizgadniba)

**Latvian:**

> Bērna vārda vai uzvārda maiņai, dzīvesvietas maiņai valstī, izceļošanai uz ārvalstīm dzīvesvietai un citiem svarīgiem jautājumiem vajadzīga abu vecāku vienošanās, ja aizgādība ir kopīga.

**English:**

> The change of the child's given name or surname, the change of the child's place of residence within the State, emigration abroad for residence and other important matters shall require the agreement of both parents where the aizgadniba is joint.

**Operational note:** Article 183 is a **hard consent list** — name change, residence change within Latvia, emigration and "other important matters" require both parents' consent where aizgadniba is joint. Unilateral relocation is therefore not merely bad practice; it is a discrete civil-code violation. This is the statutory basis for interim orders preventing move-away or restoring the pre-move status quo. In cross-border cases (see the Hague 1980 layer below) art. 183 is the substantive Latvian rule that fills in "wrongful removal" under art. 3 of the 1980 Convention.

### Civilprocesa likums art. 620 (relevant paragraphs) — Enforcement of decisions concerning the child

**Latvian (working extract, in the version applied to saskarsme enforcement):**

> Tiesu izpildītājs izpilda tiesas nolēmumu par bērna nodošanu, saskarsmes tiesību izlietošanu vai citu bērna interesēm nozīmīgu darbību piedalība ar bāriņtiesas pārstāvi. Ja pienākumos noteiktais netiek pildīts, tiesu izpildītājs var uzlikt naudas sodu, un naudas sodus var uzlikt atkārtoti līdz nolēmuma izpildei.

**English:**

> The bailiff (tiesu izpildītājs) shall enforce the court's decision on the handover of the child, the exercise of contact rights or other matters significant to the child's interests with the participation of a representative of the orphan's court (barintiesa). Where the duties fixed are not performed, the bailiff may impose a fine, and fines may be imposed repeatedly until the decision is complied with.

**Operational note:** Enforcement of Latvian contact orders is a two-agency operation — the bailiff at the coercive layer and the barintiesa (orphan's court, which under Latvian law is an administrative body with child-protection jurisdiction, not a court in the judicial sense) at the child-protection layer. Fines under Civilprocesa likums art. 620 are cumulative; the barintiesa's parallel administrative track can independently escalate to residence-change proposals.

## Structural Reading

Nine moving parts:

1. **Art. 177** — Aizgadniba is a composite right (care + supervision + representation + residence-fixing).
2. **Art. 178** — Parents' rights are equal; exercise is joint; disagreement goes to court.
3. **Art. 178.1** — After separation, joint aizgadniba continues by presumption; sole aizgadniba requires impossibility *and* best interests.
4. **Art. 178.2** — Even under sole aizgadniba, the non-residential parent keeps contact + information rights and maintenance duty.
5. **Art. 179** — Under joint aizgadniba, representation of the child is joint; unilateral representation by the residential parent is *ultra vires*.
6. **Art. 181** — The child has the *primary* contact right (paragraph 1); the parent has a paired right-and-duty (paragraph 2); information is a free-standing right (paragraph 3).
7. **Art. 182** — Residential parent has a *positive duty to promote* contact (paragraph 1); using aizgadniba to obstruct is abuse of right (paragraph 2); court fixes contact where agreement fails (paragraph 3).
8. **Art. 183** — Name change, residence change within Latvia, emigration, and "other important matters" require both parents' consent under joint aizgadniba.
9. **Civilprocesa likums art. 620** — Two-agency enforcement (bailiff + barintiesa); cumulative fines.

The scheme reads as the strongest **positive-duty** formulation in the Baltic block: art. 182(1) is a promotion duty, not a non-obstruction duty; art. 178.1(1) presumes continuation; art. 181(3) makes information a stand-alone right.

## Augstākā tiesa (Supreme Court) — Interpretive Line

Working references to the Augstākā tiesa civil-law department (Civillietu departaments) rulings on aizgadniba and saskarsme are collected on at.gov.lv. The doctrinal line is stable; specific case-numbering (SKC-XXX/YYYY) should be verified against InfoLex or the AT publication of judgments before pleading. Recurring themes:

- **Art. 182(1) as an operational duty of promotion.** The AT reads the "veicināt" (promote) verb as requiring affirmative facilitation — not merely permission. The residential parent's default posture must be one of enabling contact; silence or delay operates against the residential parent in the evidentiary sense.
- **Art. 178.1(3) — the double conjunction.** The AT has repeatedly held that "impossibility of joint aizgadniba" is a fact-finding standard separate from "in the child's interests", and that the trial court's judgment must reason both conjuncts on the face of the ruling. Absence of reasoning on either conjunct is a ground of cassation.
- **Child's expressed refusal and induced influence.** The AT reads art. 181(1) together with art. 182(2) (prohibition of abuse of aizgadniba) to require the trial court to assess whether an expressed refusal reflects the child's own view or an induced view; a formulaic reliance on the child's stated preference without such assessment is reviewable.
- **Barintiesa recommendations are not binding, but reasoned departure is required.** The barintiesa's role under art. 620 gives its report evidentiary weight; the AT has held that the trial court is not bound by barintiesa recommendations but must give reasons for departing.

## Satversmes tiesa (Constitutional Court) — Framing

Latvia's Constitutional Court (Satversmes tiesa) has held that the State's obligations under **Satversme art. 110** (protection of the family) run in parallel with Article 8 ECHR. The framing is the same as Lithuania's KT and Estonia's Riigikohus: a formally correct contact order that is not enforced does not discharge the State's obligation. Effectiveness of enforcement is a constitutional as well as a Convention requirement, and repeated failures at the coercive level (art. 620) generate a Satversme art. 110 defect independent of the underlying contact regime.

## ECHR Application to Latvia

Latvia is a party to the ECHR (ratified 1997). Relevant Article 8 jurisprudence for alienation practice:

- **X v Latvia [GC] (2013)** — the Grand Chamber Hague-1980 return decision that anchored the "genuine consideration" and "arguable allegations" doctrine within Article 8. Domestic effect: Latvian courts must reason the Article 13(1)(b) exception on the face of the return decision.
- **Hokkanen v Finland (1994)**, **Ignaccolo-Zenide v Romania (2000)**, **Sahin v Germany [GC] (2003)**, **Neulinger and Shuruk v Switzerland [GC] (2010)**, **Improta v Italy (2017)** — the general positive-obligations line, applied by Latvian courts as binding interpretive authority through the Satversmes tiesa's parallel-obligations framing.

## Baltic Comparative Block

- **Latvia — Civillikums (this entry).** Art. 178.1(1) presumption of continuation; art. 181(3) free-standing information right; art. 182(1) positive duty of promotion; art. 183 hard consent list.
- **Estonia — Perekonnaseadus 2010.** See /case-law/estonia/perekonnaseadus-2010-arts-116-118-137-143-verbatim/. § 143(2) ("acts which harm the relations") is the textually broadest anti-obstruction formulation in the block. § 137(2) allows matter-specific decision-right transfer without stripping the composite.
- **Lithuania — Civilinis kodeksas 2000.** See /case-law/lithuania/civilinis-kodeksas-3-156-3-178-tevu-valdzia-verbatim/. § 3.175(2) codifies statutory maximisation of contact; § 3.174(2)(3) codifies the express friendly-parent factor.

The Baltic block reads as a coherent drafting tradition rather than three parallel adoptions. Latvia's Civillikums is the pre-Soviet civil-code base (1937); Estonia's Perekonnaseadus is a standalone modern statute (2010); Lithuania's Civilinis kodeksas is an integrated civil-code family book (2000). All three converge on the joint-exercise-plus-continuation-plus-anti-obstruction architecture, but each contributes a distinct textual feature (Latvia: positive promotion; Estonia: broad harm formulation; Lithuania: maximisation and friendly-parent factor). In cross-border practice the three are cited together as the "Baltic positive-framing block".

## Cross-Border Framework

- **Brussels IIb (Regulation (EU) 2019/1111)** applies to Latvia as an EU Member State since 1 August 2022 (superseding Brussels IIa).
- **Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction 1980** — Latvia is a party; central authority is the **Tieslietu ministrija** (Ministry of Justice).
- **Hague Convention on Jurisdiction, Applicable Law, Recognition, Enforcement and Cooperation in Respect of Parental Responsibility and Measures for the Protection of Children 1996** — Latvia is a party.
- **1980 European Convention on Recognition and Enforcement of Decisions concerning Custody of Children** — in force for Latvia.
- **Bilateral cooperation** — Post-2004 EU emigration produced substantial Latvian diaspora populations in the UK, Ireland, Germany, Norway, Sweden and the United States, generating cross-border alienation case-flow in those forums. Post-2022 humanitarian intake from Ukraine adds a further cross-border dimension.

## Practical Application

### Motion Language (Latvian, statute-anchored)

> "Atbildētājs sistemātiski nepilda Civillikuma 182. panta pirmās daļas noteikto veicināšanas pienākumu un izmanto aizgādības tiesības pretēji bērna interesēm Civillikuma 182. panta otrās daļas nozīmē. Turklāt atbildētājs vienpusēji ir mainījis bērna dzīvesvietu bez pieteicēja piekrišanas Civillikuma 183. panta nozīmē. Pieteicējs lūdz tiesu (i) piemērot Civilprocesa likuma 620. pantā noteiktos naudas sodus; (ii) noteikt saskarsmes kārtību Civillikuma 182. panta trešās daļas kārtībā; (iii) ja nepieciešams, izlemt par atsevišķu aizgādību pieteicējam Civillikuma 178.1 panta trešās daļas kārtībā, ievērojot atbildētāja rīcības nesavietojamību ar bērna interesēm."

### English (working translation for pleading-support purposes)

> "The respondent systematically fails to perform the duty of promotion fixed by Civil Code art. 182(1) and uses aizgadniba rights contrary to the interests of the child within the meaning of Civil Code art. 182(2). Furthermore, the respondent has unilaterally changed the child's place of residence without the applicant's consent within the meaning of Civil Code art. 183. The applicant requests that the court (i) impose the fines fixed by Civil Procedure Law art. 620; (ii) fix the order of contact under Civil Code art. 182(3); (iii) if necessary, decide on sole aizgadniba with the applicant under Civil Code art. 178.1(3), having regard to the incompatibility of the respondent's conduct with the child's interests."

### Pleading Sequence

1. Establish joint aizgadniba as the presumptive state via **art. 178** and **art. 178.1(1)**.
2. Anchor the composite scope of aizgadniba via **art. 177(2)–(4)** and the joint-representation requirement via **art. 179**.
3. Document breach of the *positive* promotion duty under **art. 182(1)** — not merely non-obstruction, promotion.
4. Frame the pattern as abuse of aizgadniba under **art. 182(2)**.
5. Where relevant, plead breach of **art. 183** (unilateral name change, unilateral residence change, unilateral emigration).
6. Plead **art. 181(3)** *separately* as a free-standing information-rights claim if information starvation is present.
7. Run **Civilprocesa likums art. 620** for coercive fines in parallel with the substantive motion.
8. Bring the substantive motion for contact-order fixing under **art. 182(3)** and, in extremis, for sole aizgadniba to the applicant under **art. 178.1(3)** with express reasoning on both the "impossibility of joint aizgadniba" conjunct and the "child's interests" conjunct.
9. Anchor the Article 8 layer with **X v Latvia [GC] 2013** and the general Strasbourg positive-obligations line.
