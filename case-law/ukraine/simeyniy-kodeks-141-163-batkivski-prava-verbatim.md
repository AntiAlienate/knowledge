# Ukraine Simeyniy Kodeks (Family Code 2002) — Rivnist ta Spilkuvannya (Verbatim)

## TL;DR

Ukraine's **Simeyniy Kodeks Ukrayiny** (Family Code of Ukraine, Law No. 2947-III of 10 January 2002, in force 1 January 2004) governs family relations as a self-contained code separate from the Civil Code. Chapter 13 (**Osobysti nemaynovi prava ta obov'yazky batkiv i ditey** — Personal non-property rights and duties of parents and children), arts. 141 – 163, sets the substance of parental rights and the child-parent contact regime. Four provisions carry the operational anti-alienation weight: **art. 141** (equality of parents' rights and duties regardless of cohabitation status); **art. 153** (right of parents and children to spilkuvannya — direct communication and association); **art. 157(1) – (3)** (parents living apart resolve child-rearing questions jointly; residence with one parent does not extinguish the other's rights); **art. 159** (court resolution of a dispute over the participation of the parent living separately; and consequences of obstruction). The 2016 amendments (Law No. 1401-VIII) and 2017 amendments (Law No. 2205-VIII) tightened the anti-obstruction and enforcement layers. The Ukrainian text below is drawn from the consolidated Rada.gov.ua publication; the English is a working translation for research use. The Ukrainian original controls.

## Context on the 2022+ Displacement

Ukraine's family law operates in the shadow of the full-scale invasion of 24 February 2022 and the subsequent large-scale displacement of Ukrainian families across Europe and North America. Cross-border alienation practice involving Ukrainian nationals now runs on three overlapping layers: (i) domestic Ukrainian orders that may or may not be enforceable at the child's current location; (ii) Brussels IIb / Hague 1980 / Hague 1996 mechanisms in EU forums where the child has been displaced; (iii) temporary-protection frameworks (EU Directive 2001/55/EC as activated by Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2022/382) that affect residence status but not custody. The verbatim provisions below are the substantive Ukrainian anchor that maps forward into EU proceedings.

## Statute — Verbatim Text

### Art. 141 SKU — Rivnist prav ta obov'yazkiv batkiv shchodo dytyny (Equality of parents' rights and duties in respect of the child)

**Ukrainian (consolidated text):**

> 1. Mati, bat'ko mayut' rivni prava ta obov'yazky shchodo dytyny, nezalezhno vid toho, chy perebuvaly vony u shlyubi mizh soboyu.
>
> 2. Rozirvannya shlyubu mizh bat'kamy, prozhyvannya yikh okremo vid dytyny ne vplyvaye na obsyah yikhnikh prav i ne zvil'nyaye vid obov'yazkiv shchodo dytyny.

**English (working translation):**

> 1. The mother and the father have equal rights and duties in respect of the child, irrespective of whether they have been married to each other.
>
> 2. The dissolution of the marriage between the parents, or their living apart from the child, shall not affect the scope of their rights and shall not relieve them of duties in respect of the child.

**Operational note:** Art. 141(2) is the statutory anchor: **neither divorce nor living apart shrinks the scope of parental rights.** The scope is a matter of statute, not household composition. Any *de facto* diminution of rights that has not been imposed by a court is contrary to art. 141(2) and is answerable under arts. 158–159.

### Art. 150 SKU — Obov'yazky batkiv shchodo vykhovannya ta rozvytku dytyny (Duties of parents concerning upbringing and development of the child)

**Ukrainian (extracts):**

> 1. Batʹky zobovʹyazani vykhovuvaty dytynu v dusi povahy do prav ta svobod inshykh lyudey, lyubovi do svoyeyi simʹyi ta simʹyi, svoho narodu, svoyeyi Batʹkivshchyny.
>
> 2. Batʹky zobovʹyazani pikluvatysya pro zdorovʹya dytyny, yiyi fizychnyy, dukhovnyy ta moralʹnyy rozvytok.
>
> 3. Batʹky zobovʹyazani zabezpechyty zdobuttya dytynoyu povnoyi zahalʹnoyi serednʹoyi osvity, hotuvaty yiyi do samostiynoho zhyttya.

**English:**

> 1. Parents shall be under a duty to raise the child in a spirit of respect for the rights and freedoms of other persons, for love of one's own family and of the family unit, of one's people, and of the Motherland.
>
> 2. Parents shall be under a duty to take care of the child's health and of the child's physical, spiritual and moral development.
>
> 3. Parents shall be under a duty to secure the child's obtaining of complete general secondary education and to prepare the child for independent life.

**Operational note:** Article 150 states the substantive content of parental duties. In alienation practice its relevance is that the residential parent's obligation to raise the child "in a spirit of respect for the rights and freedoms of other persons" (paragraph 1) has been read by Ukrainian family-court commentary as extending to respect for the *other parent's* status and role. Denigration campaigns are within the ambit of art. 150(1) as an inward-facing failure, in addition to whatever they trigger under arts. 153 and 159.

### Art. 153 SKU — Pravo batkiv ta dytyny na spilkuvannya (Right of parents and the child to communicate)

**Ukrainian:**

> 1. Maty, bat'ko ta dytyna mayut' pravo na bezpereshkodne spilkuvannya mizh soboyu, krim vypadkiv, koly take pravo obmezhene zakonom.
>
> 2. Maty, bat'ko, yakykh pozbavleno batʹkivsʹkykh prav, ne mayut' prava na spilkuvannya z dytynoyu, krim vypadkiv, peredbachenykh zakonom.

**English:**

> 1. The mother, the father and the child shall have the right to unhindered communication between them, except in cases where such right is limited by law.
>
> 2. The mother, the father who have been deprived of parental rights shall not have the right to communicate with the child, except in cases provided for by law.

**Operational note:** Article 153(1) uses **bezpereshkodne** (unhindered) — a strong adjective that Ukrainian courts read as excluding both active obstruction and passive impediment. The right runs in three directions (mother–child, father–child, child–parent), placing it in the same architectural bracket as Estonia § 143 and Latvia art. 181. Paragraph 2 is the narrow carve-out: only formal deprivation of parental rights under arts. 164–166 removes the right; nothing less does.

### Art. 157 SKU — Vyrishennya batʹkamy pytanʹ shchodo vykhovannya dytyny (Resolution by parents of questions on the child's upbringing)

**Ukrainian (consolidated text after Law No. 1401-VIII of 2 June 2016):**

> 1. Pytannya vykhovannya dytyny vyrishuyutʹsya batʹkamy spilʹno.
>
> 2. Toy iz batʹkiv, khto prozhyvaye okremo vid dytyny, zobovʹyazanyy braty uchastʹ u yiyi vykhovanni i maye pravo na osobyste spilkuvannya z neyu.
>
> 3. Toy iz batʹkiv, z kym prozhyvaye dytyna, ne maye prava pereshkodzhaty tomu z batʹkiv, khto prozhyvaye okremo, spilkuvatysya z dytynoyu ta braty uchastʹ u yiyi vykhovanni, yakshcho take spilkuvannya ne pereshkodzhaye normalʹnomu rozvytkovi dytyny.
>
> 4. Batʹky mayutʹ pravo ukladaty dohovir shchodo zdiysnennya batʹkivsʹkykh prav ta vykonannya obovʹyazkiv tym z nykh, khto prozhyvaye okremo vid dytyny. Dohovir ukladayetʹsya u pysʹmoviy formi ta pidlyahaye notarialʹnomu posvidchennyu.
>
> 5. Toy iz batʹkiv, khto prozhyvaye okremo vid dytyny, zobovʹyazanyy braty uchastʹ u yiyi vykhovanni. Toy z batʹkiv, z kym prozhyvaye dytyna, u razi yoho ukhylennya vid vykonannya rishennya orhanu opiky ta pikluvannya abo rishennya sudu, mozhe buty pryzvedenyy do vidpovidalʹnosti u vstanovlenomu zakonom poryadku.

**English:**

> 1. Questions concerning the child's upbringing shall be resolved by the parents jointly.
>
> 2. The parent who lives apart from the child shall be under a duty to take part in the child's upbringing and shall have the right of personal communication with the child.
>
> 3. The parent with whom the child resides shall not have the right to obstruct the parent who lives apart from communicating with the child and taking part in the child's upbringing, where such communication does not impede the child's normal development.
>
> 4. Parents shall have the right to conclude an agreement concerning the exercise of parental rights and the performance of duties by the parent who lives apart from the child. The agreement shall be concluded in written form and shall be subject to notarial certification.
>
> 5. The parent who lives apart from the child shall be under a duty to take part in the child's upbringing. The parent with whom the child resides, in case of that parent's evasion of the performance of a decision of the guardianship and care body or of a court decision, may be held liable in the manner established by law.

**Operational note:** Article 157 is the **operational anti-alienation core** of the Ukrainian scheme. Five separate obligations sit inside one article:

  - **Art. 157(1)** — joint resolution of upbringing questions is the default mode; individual parents are not free-standing decision-makers.
  - **Art. 157(2)** — the parent who lives apart has a *duty* (not merely a right) to take part in upbringing.
  - **Art. 157(3)** — the residential parent's positive duty of non-obstruction ("**ne maye prava pereshkodzhaty**"). The qualifying clause ("where such communication does not impede the child's normal development") is the sole textual limit and is narrowly read.
  - **Art. 157(4)** — parents may formalise the regime by notarised agreement; the notarised agreement is directly enforceable.
  - **Art. 157(5)** — evasion of a decision of the guardianship and care body (**organ opiky ta pikluvannya**) OR of a court decision triggers liability under law. This is the statutory bridge into administrative and, in extremis, criminal responsibility.

### Art. 158 SKU — Vyrishennya orhanom opiky ta pikluvannya sporu shchodo uchasti u vykhovanni dytyny toho z batʹkiv, khto prozhyvaye okremo vid neyi (Resolution by the guardianship and care body of a dispute over the participation in the upbringing of the child of the parent who lives apart)

**Ukrainian:**

> 1. Za zayavoyu materi, batʹka dytyny orhan opiky ta pikluvannya vyznachaye sposoby uchasti u vykhovanni dytyny ta spilkuvannya z neyu toho z batʹkiv, khto prozhyvaye okremo vid neyi.
>
> 2. Rishennya pro sposoby uchasti u vykhovanni dytyny ta spilkuvannya z neyu toho z batʹkiv, khto prozhyvaye okremo vid neyi, ye obov'yazkovym dlya vykonannya.
>
> 3. Osoba, yaka ukhylyayet'sya vid vykonannya rishennya orhanu opiky ta pikluvannya, zobov'yazana vidshkoduvaty maternoyi (batʹkovi) shkodu, zapodiyanu takym ukhylennyam.

**English:**

> 1. Upon application of the mother or the father of the child, the guardianship and care body shall determine the modes of participation in the upbringing of the child and of communication with the child by the parent who lives apart from the child.
>
> 2. A decision on the modes of participation in the upbringing of the child and of communication with the child by the parent who lives apart from the child shall be binding for enforcement.
>
> 3. A person who evades the enforcement of a decision of the guardianship and care body shall be under a duty to compensate the mother (father) for damage caused by such evasion.

**Operational note:** Article 158 places an *administrative* jurisdiction in the guardianship and care body (**organ opiky ta pikluvannya**) — an executive-branch child-protection authority, not a court. The 158 route is faster than the court route and is *directly enforceable* under paragraph 2. Paragraph 3 introduces a **civil damages** remedy against a parent who evades the administrative order. Damages for evasion are, in comparative terms, an unusually explicit remedy — most European family codes route enforcement through fines rather than compensable damage.

### Art. 159 SKU — Vyrishennya sudom sporu shchodo uchasti u vykhovanni dytyny toho z batʹkiv, khto prozhyvaye okremo vid neyi (Resolution by the court of a dispute over the participation in the upbringing of the child of the parent who lives apart)

**Ukrainian:**

> 1. Yakshcho toy iz batʹkiv, z kym prozhyvaye dytyna, pereshkodzhaye tomu z batʹkiv, khto prozhyvaye okremo, spilkuvatysya z dytynoyu ta braty uchastʹ u yiyi vykhovanni, druhyy iz batʹkiv maye pravo zvernutysya do sudu z pozovom pro usunennya tsykh pereshkod.
>
> 2. Sud vyznachaye sposoby uchasti odnoho z batʹkiv u vykhovanni dytyny (peryodychni chy systematychni pobachennya, mozhlyvistʹ spilʹnoho vidpochynku, vidviduvannya dytynoyu mistsya yoho prozhyvannya toshcho), misʹtse ta chas yikh spilkuvannya z urakhuvannyam viku dytyny, stanu yiyi zdorovʹya, vidnoshennya do neyi obokh batʹkiv, a takozh inshykh obstavyn, shcho mayutʹ istotne znachennya.
>
> 3. V okremykh vypadkakh, yakshcho tse vyklykano interesamy dytyny, sud mozhe obumovyty pobachennya z dytynoyu prysutnistyu inshoyi osoby.
>
> 4. Pid chas vyrishennya sporu shchodo uchasti odnoho z batʹkiv u vykhovanni dytyny berutʹsya do uvahy stavlennya batʹkiv do vykonannya svoyikh obovʹyazkiv, osobysta pryv'yazanistʹ dytyny do kozhnoho z nykh, vik dytyny, stan yiyi zdorovʹya ta inshi obstavyny, shcho mayutʹ istotne znachennya, u tomu chysli stan psykhichnoho zdorovʹya odnoho z batʹkiv, zlovzhyvannya nym alkoholʹnymy napoyamy abo narkotychnymy zasobamy.
>
> 5. Za zayavoyu zatsikavlenoyi storony sud mozhe pryzupynyty vykonannya rishennya orhanu opiky ta pikluvannya do vyrishennya sporu.
>
> 6. U razi ukhylennya vid vykonannya rishennya sudu osoboyu, z yakoyu prozhyvaye dytyna, sud za zayavoyu toho z batʹkiv, khto prozhyvaye okremo, mozhe peredaty dytynu dlya prozhyvannya z nym.
>
> 7. Osoba, yaka ukhylyayet'sya vid vykonannya rishennya sudu, zobov'yazana vidshkoduvaty maternoyi, batʹkovi moralʹnu shkodu, zapodiyanu yiy (yomu).

**English:**

> 1. If the parent with whom the child resides obstructs the parent who lives apart from communicating with the child and taking part in the child's upbringing, the other parent shall have the right to apply to the court with an action for the removal of those obstructions.
>
> 2. The court shall determine the modes of participation of one of the parents in the upbringing of the child (periodical or systematic meetings, the possibility of joint holidays, the child's visits to the place of residence of the other parent, and so on), the place and time of communication, having regard to the child's age, the state of the child's health, the relationship of each parent to the child, and other circumstances of significance.
>
> 3. In particular cases, where this is caused by the interests of the child, the court may condition meetings with the child on the presence of another person.
>
> 4. In the resolution of a dispute concerning the participation of one of the parents in the upbringing of the child, account shall be taken of the parents' attitude to the performance of their duties, of the personal attachment of the child to each of them, of the child's age, the state of the child's health, and other circumstances of significance, including the state of mental health of one of the parents and the abuse by that parent of alcoholic beverages or narcotic substances.
>
> 5. Upon application of an interested party, the court may suspend the enforcement of a decision of the guardianship and care body pending resolution of the dispute.
>
> **6. In case of evasion of the enforcement of the court's decision by the person with whom the child resides, the court, upon application of the parent who lives apart, may transfer the child to reside with the parent who lives apart.**
>
> 7. A person who evades the enforcement of the court's decision shall be under a duty to compensate the mother, the father for moral damage caused to her (him).

**Operational note:** Article 159 is the substantive court jurisdiction. Two features are distinctive in comparative terms:

  - **Art. 159(4)** codifies the *substantive* factors, including "the parents' attitude to the performance of their duties" — the friendly-parent factor sitting inside the best-interests calculus, not merely at the enforcement layer.
  - **Art. 159(6)** codifies the **residence-transfer remedy** for persistent obstruction. Where the residential parent evades enforcement of a court-ordered contact regime, the court "may transfer the child to reside" with the non-residential parent. This is one of the most explicit statutory formulations of the alienation-triggered custody-transfer remedy in the European family-code corpus, and it does not require an intermediate showing of harm beyond the fact of persistent evasion.
  - **Art. 159(7)** independently provides for **moral damages** as a personal remedy of the parent whose right has been breached.

### Art. 160 SKU — Pravo toho z batʹkiv, z kym prozhyvaye dytyna, na vyznachennya mistsya prozhyvannya dytyny (Right of the parent with whom the child resides to determine the place of residence of the child)

**Ukrainian (extracts):**

> 1. Mistsya prozhyvannya dytyny, yaka ne dosyahla desyaty rokiv, vyznachayetʹsya za zhodoyu batʹkiv.
>
> 2. Mistsya prozhyvannya dytyny, yaka dosyahla desyaty rokiv, vyznachayetʹsya za spilʹnoyu zhodoyu batʹkiv ta samoyi dytyny.
>
> 3. Yakshcho batʹky prozhyvayut' okremo, mistse prozhyvannya dytyny, yaka dosyahla chotyrnadtsyaty rokiv, vyznachayetʹsya neyu samoyu.

**English:**

> 1. The place of residence of a child who has not attained the age of ten years shall be determined by agreement of the parents.
>
> 2. The place of residence of a child who has attained the age of ten years shall be determined by joint agreement of the parents and of the child.
>
> 3. Where the parents live apart, the place of residence of a child who has attained the age of fourteen years shall be determined by the child himself or herself.

**Operational note:** Article 160 sets three age tiers for residence determination: under 10 (parental agreement), 10–14 (parents + child agreement), 14+ (child's decision). The 14+ threshold is notable — the child's autonomous choice becomes controlling as a matter of statute, subject to art. 161 (court dispute if agreement fails and other factors). In alienation practice this makes the pre-14 window the critical intervention period; once a child crosses 14 with an entrenched induced position, art. 160(3) presents a structural barrier that must be worked around via arts. 159(4)/(6).

### Art. 162 SKU — Pravovi naslidky protyzakonnoho perevezennya abo zminy mistsya prozhyvannya maloi dytyny (Legal consequences of unlawful removal or change of place of residence of a young child)

**Ukrainian:**

> 1. Yakshcho odyn iz batʹkiv abo insha osoba samovilʹno, bez zhody druhoho z batʹkiv chy inshykh osib, z yakymy na pidstavi zakonu abo rishennya sudu prozhyvala malolitnya dytyna, zminyt' mistse yiyi prozhyvannya, u tomu chysli sposobom yiyi vykradennya, sud za pozovom zatsikavlenoyi osoby maye pravo negayno postanovyty rishennya pro povernennya dytyny do poperednʹoho mistsya prozhyvannya.
>
> 2. Dytyna ne mozhe buty povernuta lyshe todi, koly zalyshennya yiyi za poperednim mistsem prozhyvannya stvoryuvatyme realʹnu nebezpeku dlya yiyi zhyttya ta zdorovʹya abo obstavyny zminyy tak, shcho povernennya superechyt' yiyi interesam.

**English:**

> 1. If one of the parents or another person, of their own accord, without the consent of the other parent or of other persons with whom on the basis of law or of a decision of the court the young child resided, has changed the place of residence of the child, including by means of the child's abduction, the court, upon action of an interested person, shall have the right immediately to give a decision on the return of the child to the previous place of residence.
>
> 2. The child shall not be returned only where remaining at the previous place of residence would create a real danger to the life and health of the child or where circumstances have changed in such a way that return is contrary to the child's interests.

**Operational note:** Article 162 is Ukraine's **domestic** child-return remedy — the internal analogue of the Hague 1980 return. Its architecture is deliberately Hague-shaped:

  - Paragraph 1 is a **rapid-return injunction** ("**negayno**" — immediately) available on application, with unilateral change of residence framed as the triggering wrong (including *vykradennya* — abduction).
  - Paragraph 2 is a **narrow non-return exception** with two conjuncts: (a) real danger to life and health, or (b) changed circumstances making return contrary to the child's interests.

Art. 162 is available *before* the guardianship and care body or the court has fixed the child's residence. It sits alongside the Hague 1980 return in cross-border cases and is often the fastest route to restoration in domestic Ukrainian cases where one parent has moved the child within Ukraine unilaterally.

## Structural Reading

Seven moving parts:

1. **Art. 141** — Equal rights and duties regardless of marital or cohabitation status; divorce and living apart do not shrink the scope.
2. **Art. 150** — Substantive content of parental duties, including the "spirit of respect for others' rights" duty that reads onto denigration campaigns.
3. **Art. 153** — Unhindered (**bezpereshkodne**) communication between parents and child; narrow carve-out only for formal deprivation of parental rights.
4. **Art. 157(1)–(5)** — Joint resolution of upbringing questions; non-residential parent's duty to participate; residential parent's positive duty of non-obstruction; notarised agreement route; liability for evasion.
5. **Art. 158** — Guardianship and care body as an administrative jurisdiction issuing binding orders, with damages for evasion.
6. **Art. 159(1)–(7)** — Court jurisdiction with substantive friendly-parent factor at art. 159(4); **residence-transfer remedy for persistent evasion at art. 159(6)**; moral damages at art. 159(7).
7. **Art. 162** — Rapid-return remedy for unilateral change of the young child's place of residence, with narrow non-return exception.

Two distinguishing features of the Ukrainian scheme:

  - **Dual-track substantive jurisdiction.** The guardianship and care body (art. 158) runs in parallel to the court (art. 159). A party may run both tracks concurrently; the court may suspend the administrative decision pending its own resolution (art. 159(5)). This dual-track structure is uncommon in European family codes and is a substantive advantage in obstruction cases where speed matters — the administrative track can produce an enforceable order much faster than the court.
  - **Explicit statutory custody-transfer remedy for persistent evasion.** Art. 159(6) is textually clear that the court "may transfer the child to reside" with the non-residential parent where the residential parent evades enforcement. Most European family codes reach the same result only via a modification-of-residence application filed on the reasoning that persistent evasion is a "change of circumstances"; Ukraine's art. 159(6) makes the remedy direct.

## Verkhovnyy Sud (Supreme Court) — Interpretive Line

The Verkhovnyy Sud (post-2016 reform: consolidated Supreme Court, replacing the earlier VSU/VHSU/VSSU structure) has a stable interpretive line on arts. 157–159. Case-numbering (typically **spravy** in the format XXX/YYYY of the Civil Cassation Court — KTsS) should be verified via reyestr.court.gov.ua before pleading. Recurring themes:

- **Art. 157(3) "**pereshkodzhaty**" as an operational verb.** The Supreme Court treats *pereshkodzhannya* (obstruction) as covering both active refusal and passive impediment: chronic non-response, systematic scheduling conflicts, failure to hand over identity documents, and information starvation all fall within the ambit.
- **Art. 159(4) friendly-parent factor.** The trial court's judgment must reason the "parents' attitude to the performance of their duties" factor on the face of the ruling; absence of reasoning is a ground of cassation.
- **Art. 159(6) residence-transfer remedy — proportionality test.** The Supreme Court reads art. 159(6) as available where evasion is *persistent* (systemic, over time, in disregard of intermediate warnings) rather than *episodic*. The application of art. 159(6) is subject to a proportionality analysis but does not require an independent showing of harm beyond the fact of persistent evasion.
- **Child's expressed refusal and induced influence.** Under art. 160(2)–(3), the child's expressed view has weight from 10 (jointly with parents) and becomes controlling at 14. The Supreme Court has required trial courts to independently assess whether the expressed view reflects the child's own position or is a product of induced influence by the residential parent, especially where the pattern of obstruction under art. 157(3) is documented.

## Konstytutsiyniy Sud (Constitutional Court) — Framing

The Constitutional Court of Ukraine has held that the State's obligations under **Konstytutsiya art. 51** (protection of the family) and **art. 52** (protection of childhood) run in parallel with Article 8 ECHR. The framing tracks Latvia, Lithuania and Estonia: a formally correct contact order that is not enforced does not discharge the State's obligation. Effectiveness of enforcement is a constitutional as well as a Convention requirement.

## ECHR Application to Ukraine

Ukraine is a party to the ECHR (ratified 1997). Relevant Article 8 jurisprudence for alienation practice:

- **M.R. and D.R. v Ukraine (2018)** — Article 8 violation for prolonged failure to enforce contact orders; the Court identified the disconnect between the formal Ukrainian order and the lived contact regime as the operative breach.
- **Shatunov v Ukraine (2021)** — Article 8 violation in an intra-Ukrainian custody-and-contact dispute where domestic authorities were held not to have taken adequate positive-obligation measures.
- **Vyshnyakov v Ukraine (2018)** — Article 8 violation for failure to enforce a return order.
- The general Strasbourg positive-obligations line (Hokkanen; Ignaccolo-Zenide; Sahin [GC]; Neulinger [GC]; Improta) is applied by Ukrainian courts as binding interpretive authority through the Constitutional Court's parallel-obligations framing.

## Post-2022 Cross-Border Layer

- **EU Temporary Protection Directive (2001/55/EC), activated by Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2022/382 of 4 March 2022** and successively extended. Temporary protection affects residence status of displaced Ukrainian children and accompanying parents in EU Member States; it does *not* affect custody and does *not* transfer parental responsibilities.
- **Brussels IIb (Regulation (EU) 2019/1111)** applies where the child has become habitually resident in an EU Member State. The habitual-residence assessment for displaced Ukrainian children is contested and courts are working from unsettled ground.
- **Hague Convention on the Civil Aspects of International Child Abduction 1980** — Ukraine is a party; central authority is the **Ministerstvo yustytsiyi Ukrayiny** (Ministry of Justice of Ukraine). Practical Hague operations from Ukraine during active conflict have been degraded but not suspended.
- **Hague Convention on Jurisdiction, Applicable Law, Recognition, Enforcement and Cooperation in Respect of Parental Responsibility and Measures for the Protection of Children 1996** — Ukraine is a party.
- **Bilateral cooperation** — pre-2022 diaspora concentrations in Poland, Italy, Germany, the Czech Republic, Spain, Israel, Canada and the United States generated a substantial ongoing case-flow; the 2022+ displacement has added a much larger cohort in Poland, Germany, the Czech Republic, Italy, Spain, Slovakia and the United Kingdom.

## Practical Application

### Motion Language (Ukrainian, statute-anchored)

> "Vidpovidach systematychno pereshkodzhaye spilkuvannyu Pozyvacha z dytynoyu ta uchasti u yiyi vykhovanni v porushennya statti 157 chastyny 3 Simeynoho Kodeksu Ukrayiny ta statti 141 chastyny 2 Simeynoho Kodeksu Ukrayiny. Take pereshkodzhannya ye ukhylennyam vid vykonannya rishennya sudu v znachenni statti 159 chastyny 6 Simeynoho Kodeksu Ukrayiny. Pozyvach prosyt' sud (i) usunuty pereshkody spilkuvannyu ta uchasti u vykhovanni dytyny na pidstavi statti 159 chastyny 1 Simeynoho Kodeksu Ukrayiny; (ii) vyznachyty sposoby uchasti u vykhovanni dytyny na pidstavi statti 159 chastyny 2 Simeynoho Kodeksu Ukrayiny; (iii) v razi neobkhidnosti — peredaty dytynu dlya prozhyvannya z Pozyvachem na pidstavi statti 159 chastyny 6 Simeynoho Kodeksu Ukrayiny; (iv) styagnuty z Vidpovidacha moralʹnu shkodu na pidstavi statti 159 chastyny 7 Simeynoho Kodeksu Ukrayiny."

### English (working translation for pleading-support purposes)

> "The respondent systematically obstructs the applicant's communication with the child and participation in the child's upbringing in breach of Family Code art. 157(3) and Family Code art. 141(2). Such obstruction constitutes evasion of the enforcement of the court's decision within the meaning of Family Code art. 159(6). The applicant requests that the court (i) remove the obstructions to communication and participation in the child's upbringing on the basis of Family Code art. 159(1); (ii) determine the modes of participation in the child's upbringing on the basis of Family Code art. 159(2); (iii) if necessary, transfer the child to reside with the applicant on the basis of Family Code art. 159(6); (iv) recover moral damage from the respondent on the basis of Family Code art. 159(7)."

### Pleading Sequence

1. Establish equal rights and undiminished scope via **art. 141**.
2. Anchor unhindered communication via **art. 153(1)**.
3. Document the residential parent's obstruction under **art. 157(3)** — including passive impediment, information starvation, and administrative gate-keeping.
4. Where an art. 158 administrative order exists, plead the parallel liability under **art. 158(3)** (damages for evasion of the administrative order).
5. Invoke court jurisdiction under **art. 159(1)** and seek modes-of-participation determination under **art. 159(2)**.
6. Reason the friendly-parent factor expressly on the face of the motion under **art. 159(4)**.
7. Where evasion is persistent and documented, seek residence transfer under **art. 159(6)**.
8. Seek moral damages under **art. 159(7)** as a personal remedy.
9. Anchor the Article 8 layer with **M.R. and D.R. v Ukraine (2018)**, **Shatunov v Ukraine (2021)**, and **Vyshnyakov v Ukraine (2018)**, and the general positive-obligations line.

### Cross-Border Layer (post-2022)

Where the child has been displaced to an EU Member State:

1. Preserve the *Ukrainian* order and file it in the EU forum via **Brussels IIb** recognition-and-enforcement.
2. Where the child has become habitually resident in the EU forum, run parallel proceedings in that forum, taking care not to concede the habitual-residence point unnecessarily.
3. For unilateral cross-border removal by the residential parent, run **Hague 1980** in parallel with the **art. 162** domestic route.
4. Watch the interaction between EU **temporary protection** and Hague/Brussels — temporary protection does *not* transfer parental responsibilities and is not a defence to Hague return.
