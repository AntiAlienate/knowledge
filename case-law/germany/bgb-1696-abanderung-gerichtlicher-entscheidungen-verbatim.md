# BGB § 1696 — Abänderung gerichtlicher Entscheidungen und gerichtlich gebilligter Vergleiche (verbatim)

**Jurisdiction:** Germany · **Citation:** *Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch* § 1696 · **Authoritative source:** [gesetze-im-internet.de — § 1696 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__1696.html)

The **modification-of-orders provision** in German family law — the structural counterpart to the Italian [art. 332 CC *reintegrazione*](../italy/codice-civile-art-332-reintegrazione-responsabilita-genitoriale.md). § 1696 BGB establishes the framework for modifying existing custody and contact decisions where (a) modification is required by *triftige* (compelling) reasons that *nachhaltig* (sustainably) affect the child's welfare, or (b) the conditions that triggered a child-protection measure under [§ 1666 BGB](bgb-1666-kindeswohlgefahrdung-verbatim.md) have ceased.

This is the rehabilitation/revision route in German PA litigation: where an alienating parent has been subject to § 1666 measures or has lost authority under § 1671, § 1696 provides the framework for restoration upon demonstrated change.

## Verbatim text (current consolidated)

### § 1696 BGB — Abänderung gerichtlicher Entscheidungen und gerichtlich gebilligter Vergleiche

> **(1)** Eine Entscheidung zum Sorge- oder Umgangsrecht oder ein gerichtlich gebilligter Vergleich ist zu ändern, wenn dies aus triftigen, das Wohl des Kindes nachhaltig berührenden Gründen angezeigt ist. Eine Entscheidung nach § 1626a Absatz 2 kann nach Maßgabe des § 1671 Absatz 1 abgeändert werden; § 1671 Absatz 4 gilt entsprechend. Die §§ 1678 Absatz 2, 1680 Absatz 2 sowie 1681 Absatz 1 und 2 bleiben unberührt.
>
> **(2)** Eine Maßnahme nach den §§ 1666 bis 1667 oder einer anderen Vorschrift des Bürgerlichen Gesetzbuchs, die nur ergriffen werden darf, wenn dies zur Abwendung einer Kindeswohlgefährdung oder zum Wohl des Kindes erforderlich ist (kindesschutzrechtliche Maßnahme), ist aufzuheben, wenn eine Gefahr für das Wohl des Kindes nicht mehr besteht oder die Erforderlichkeit der Maßnahme entfallen ist.
>
> **(3)** Eine Anordnung nach § 1632 Absatz 4 ist auf Antrag der Eltern aufzuheben, wenn die Wegnahme des Kindes von der Pflegeperson das Kindeswohl nicht gefährdet.

## English working translation

### § 1696 BGB — Modification of court decisions and court-approved settlements

> **(1)** A decision relating to parental responsibility or contact, or a court-approved settlement, must be modified where this is indicated for compelling reasons sustainably affecting the welfare of the child. A decision under § 1626a paragraph 2 may be modified in accordance with § 1671 paragraph 1; § 1671 paragraph 4 applies accordingly. §§ 1678 paragraph 2, 1680 paragraph 2, and 1681 paragraphs 1 and 2 remain unaffected.
>
> **(2)** A measure under §§ 1666 to 1667 or any other provision of the Civil Code that may be taken only where required to avert an endangerment of the child's welfare or to safeguard the welfare of the child (child-protection measure) must be revoked where a danger to the welfare of the child no longer exists or the necessity of the measure has fallen away.
>
> **(3)** An order under § 1632 paragraph 4 must be revoked on application by the parents where removal of the child from the foster carer does not endanger the child's welfare.

## Why this matters for PA

- **§ 1696 (1) — modification of contact and custody orders.** The provision is the principal route by which existing contact / custody orders are modified. The threshold — *triftige, das Wohl des Kindes nachhaltig berührende Gründe* (compelling reasons sustainably affecting the child's welfare) — is intentionally high. Modification is not available on the basis of routine change; the change must be substantial and welfare-affecting.
- **§ 1696 (2) — mandatory revocation of child-protection measures.** Where a § 1666 measure (e.g. partial removal of parental authority, contact prohibition) was imposed, the court *must* revoke the measure when the welfare-jeopardy condition is no longer present. This is the doctrinal commitment to non-permanence of welfare-protective restrictions: a parent who has been subject to § 1666 measures and who demonstrates sustained behavioural change has a statutory right to revocation, not merely a discretionary opportunity.
- **The rehabilitation route in sustained-PA cases.** Where an alienating parent has been subject to § 1666 measures, § 1696 (2) is the route back. The parent must demonstrate:
  - The Kindeswohlgefährdung is no longer present (the underlying conduct has substantially ceased)
  - The necessity of the measure has fallen away (rehabilitation is sustained, not temporary)
- **Structural parallel with Italy art. 332.** The Italian [art. 332 CC *reintegrazione*](../italy/codice-civile-art-332-reintegrazione-responsabilita-genitoriale.md) operates on the same two-prong logic: *cessate le ragioni* (reasons have ceased) + *escluso ogni pericolo* (every risk excluded). § 1696 (2) maps cleanly onto this structure.
- **The Bundesgerichtshof's threshold doctrine.** The BGH has held that the *triftige Gründe* threshold under § 1696 (1) prevents modification on the basis of party-driven re-litigation. The provision is structural protection of the existing court order; modification is reserved for cases where the welfare assessment substantively changes.

## Procedural application

- The competent court is the *Familiengericht* that issued the original order, sitting under [FamFG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/famfg/).
- The applicant bears the burden of establishing the *triftige Gründe* or the cessation of welfare-jeopardy.
- Court-appointed *Sachverständigengutachten* (psychological expert assessment) is the typical evidential pathway for the rehabilitation determination — see [expert evidence comparative](../comparative/expert-evidence-admissibility-comparative.md).
- The *Verfahrensbeistand* represents the child's interests under § 158 FamFG.
- Where the original order included third-party measures under § 1666 (4), revocation under § 1696 (2) extends to those measures.

## Use in alienating-conduct cases

In sustained alienation cases where § 1666 measures have been imposed:

- The alienating parent must demonstrate sustained behavioural change — typically multi-year demonstration through court-counselling engagement, completed parenting programmes, and absence of recurring alienating conduct
- Independent professional assessment confirming that the Kindeswohlgefährdung is no longer present
- The *Sachverständigengutachten* assesses both prongs: cessation + sustained rehabilitation
- The Bundesgerichtshof has held that revocation is mandatory under § 1696 (2) once the two-prong test is satisfied; the court does not have discretion to maintain the measure on prophylactic grounds

This is the same structural framework as Italy art. 332, Austria § 182 ABGB, and the UK s. 39 CA 1989 discharge route — see [graduated-remedy ladder comparative](../comparative/graduated-remedy-ladder-it-de-at-uk.md).
