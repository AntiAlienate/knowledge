# BGB § 1685 — Umgang des Kindes mit anderen Bezugspersonen (verbatim)

**Jurisdiction:** Germany · **Citation:** *Bürgerliches Gesetzbuch* § 1685 · **Authoritative source:** [gesetze-im-internet.de — § 1685 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__1685.html)

The **third-party contact provision** in German family law — establishing contact rights for grandparents, siblings, and close reference persons who have assumed actual responsibility for the child (*sozial-familiäre Beziehung*). § 1685 BGB operates parallel to the [§ 1684 contact-right framework](bgb-1626-1684-elterliche-sorge-umgang-verbatim.md) but for non-parent reference persons. It is doctrinally significant in PA litigation because alienating-conduct patterns frequently extend to extended-family contact — grandparents, siblings, step-relatives — which can be addressed under § 1685 independently of the parent-to-parent framework.

## Verbatim text (current consolidated)

### § 1685 BGB — Umgang des Kindes mit anderen Bezugspersonen

> **(1)** Großeltern und Geschwister haben ein Recht auf Umgang mit dem Kind, wenn dieser dem Wohl des Kindes dient.
>
> **(2)** Gleiches gilt für enge Bezugspersonen des Kindes, wenn diese für das Kind tatsächliche Verantwortung tragen oder getragen haben (sozial-familiäre Beziehung). Eine Übernahme tatsächlicher Verantwortung ist in der Regel anzunehmen, wenn die Person mit dem Kind längere Zeit in häuslicher Gemeinschaft zusammengelebt hat.
>
> **(3)** § 1684 Abs. 2 bis 4 gilt entsprechend. Eine Umgangspflegschaft nach § 1684 Abs. 3 Satz 3 bis 5 kann das Familiengericht nur anordnen, wenn die Voraussetzungen des § 1666 Abs. 1 erfüllt sind.

## English working translation

### § 1685 BGB — Contact of the child with other reference persons

> **(1)** Grandparents and siblings have a right of contact with the child where such contact serves the welfare of the child.
>
> **(2)** The same applies to close reference persons of the child where these persons bear or have borne actual responsibility for the child (socio-familial relationship). The assumption of actual responsibility is, as a rule, to be presumed where the person has lived in domestic community with the child for an extended period.
>
> **(3)** § 1684 paragraphs 2 to 4 apply correspondingly. A contact custodianship under § 1684 paragraph 3 sentences 3 to 5 may be ordered by the family court only where the conditions of § 1666 paragraph 1 are satisfied.

## Why this matters for PA

- **Grandparent and sibling contact framework.** Subsection (1) establishes an independent right of contact for grandparents and siblings, gated on the welfare-of-the-child test. In PA litigation this is operationally important: where an alienating parent has not only frustrated the targeted parent's contact but extended the conduct to block grandparent/sibling contact, § 1685 (1) provides an independent route for the grandparents/siblings to seek contact directly.
- **Subsection (2) — sozial-familiäre Beziehung.** *Close reference persons who bear or have borne actual responsibility* — the *sozial-familiäre Beziehung* concept extends contact rights beyond blood-relative grandparents/siblings to step-parents, long-term partners of the parent, and other persons who have functioned in a parental-equivalent role. The presumption of "actual responsibility" upon extended cohabitation is the operative trigger. This is doctrinally significant in modern family configurations.
- **Subsection (3) — § 1684 cross-reference.** *§ 1684 Abs. 2 bis 4 gilt entsprechend.* The Wohlverhaltensklausel under [§ 1684 (2)](bgb-1626-1684-elterliche-sorge-umgang-verbatim.md) applies — third-party contact-holders also bear the cooperation duty and the duty not to impair the child's relationship with each parent. This is structurally important: a grandparent who is being weaponised by the alienating parent (e.g. acting as the alienating parent's surrogate against the targeted parent during contact) is subject to the same Wohlverhaltensklausel.
- **Subsection (3) — Umgangspflegschaft restriction.** The *Umgangspflegschaft* (contact custodianship) under § 1684 (3) can be ordered for third-party contact *only* where the [§ 1666 Kindeswohlgefährdung](bgb-1666-kindeswohlgefahrdung-verbatim.md) conditions are met. This is a higher threshold than for parent contact — reflecting the structural distinction between parental and third-party contact-right enforcement.
- **The interaction with alienating conduct.** Where the alienating parent's conduct extends to blocking extended-family contact:
  - The grandparents/siblings can independently apply under § 1685 (1)
  - The cooperation-duty under § 1684 (2) applies to the resident parent's facilitation of § 1685 contact
  - The welfare-jeopardy threshold under § 1666 must be met for Umgangspflegschaft escalation

## Procedural application

- The competent court is the *Familiengericht* sitting under the [FamFG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/famfg/) procedural code
- The grandparent/sibling/reference person can apply independently (no parental consent required)
- The *Jugendamt* (youth welfare authority) provides welfare-assessment input
- The court applies the welfare-of-the-child test — § 1685 (1)/(2) is not absolute; it operates only where contact serves welfare
- Where the parent and the third party disagree, the court reasons through welfare assessment supported by *Sachverständigengutachten* (psychological expert assessment) — see [expert evidence comparative](../comparative/expert-evidence-admissibility-comparative.md)

## Use in alienating-conduct cases

- The leading German jurisprudence on § 1685 in alienation contexts is the BGH XII ZB line — the welfare-of-the-child test means that grandparent contact can be ordered where the welfare assessment supports it, but cannot be ordered solely on the grandparents' wishes
- Where the alienating parent is using grandparents/extended family as an extension of the alienating-conduct pattern (e.g. enlisting grandparents in disparagement of the targeted parent), § 1685 (3) + § 1684 (2) Wohlverhaltensklausel applies to the third party also
- Where the targeted parent's parents (paternal/maternal grandparents) are being blocked from contact, § 1685 (1) provides an independent procedural route — distinct from the parent-to-parent contact framework
