# Graduated-remedy ladder for alienating-conduct cases — Italy, Germany, Austria, UK comparative

**Jurisdictions covered:** Italy · Germany · Austria · England & Wales

A comparative-law map of the **graduated remedy ladder** — the structural framework that escalates from intermediate measures (counselling, supervised contact, contact custodian) through tier-2 (sole custody / parental-responsibility restriction) to tier-3 (deprivation of parental responsibility / care order) in sustained alienating-conduct cases. The four jurisdictions covered here have all developed coherent graduated frameworks but operate with structurally distinct architectures.

## The four-tier remedy structure

| Tier | Italy | Germany | Austria | UK |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1**<br/>*Counselling / mediation* | None codified (judicial *raccomandazione*) | § 156 FamFG *Erziehungsberatung* | § 107 AußStrG *Erziehungsberatung* | s. 16 Children Act *Family Assistance Order* |
| **Tier 2a**<br/>*Contact-arrangement modification* | art. 337-ter CC | § 1684 (4) BGB | § 187 (2) ABGB | s. 8 CA 1989 *contact order* |
| **Tier 2b**<br/>*Contact custodian / third party intervention* | (no direct equivalent) | § 1684 (3) BGB *Umgangspflegschaft* | (analogous via § 187 (2)) | s. 16 *Family Assistance Order* + *guardian ad litem* under s. 41 |
| **Tier 3**<br/>*Intermediate measures against alienating conduct* | **art. 333 CC** *condotta pregiudizievole* | § 1666 BGB *Kindeswohlgefährdung* (lower threshold) | (escalation via § 187 (2)) | s. 31 CA 1989 *supervision order* |
| **Tier 4**<br/>*Sole custody / residence change* | art. 337-quater CC *affidamento esclusivo* | § 1671 BGB *Alleinsorge* | § 177 ABGB *Obsorge-Übertragung* | s. 8 CA 1989 *residence order* (now *child arrangements order*) |
| **Tier 5**<br/>*Decadenza / deprivation of parental responsibility* | **art. 330 CC** *decadenza* | § 1666 BGB (severe Kindeswohlgefährdung) | § 181 ABGB *Entzug der Obsorge* | s. 31 CA 1989 *care order* |
| **Tier 6**<br/>*Rehabilitation / restoration* | **art. 332 CC** *reintegrazione* | § 1696 BGB *Abänderung gerichtlicher Entscheidungen* | § 182 ABGB *Wiederherstellung der Obsorge* | discharge of care order under s. 39 |

## Structural typology

### Italian model — explicit three-step graduated escalation

Italy is the cleanest example of an **express graduated framework**:

- art. 333 = intermediate measures (*provvedimenti convenienti*) for *condotta pregiudizievole*
- art. 330 = maximum-tier *decadenza* for *grave pregiudizio*
- art. 332 = rehabilitation route back from art. 330

The Cassazione has explicit graduated-escalation doctrine: art. 333 must usually be exhausted (or shown demonstrably inadequate) before art. 330 is sought. This is the most structurally coherent framework of the four.

### German model — Kindeswohlgefährdung as unified threshold

Germany operates a **threshold-based** framework rather than express tiers:

- § 1684 (3) BGB *Umgangspflegschaft* is available for breach of the *Wohlverhaltensklausel* without requiring a Kindeswohlgefährdung finding
- § 1671 BGB sole-custody-on-application is independent of the welfare-jeopardy threshold
- § 1666 BGB engages once the Kindeswohlgefährdung threshold is met — and offers the full spectrum from milder measures (e.g. supervised contact, prohibition on specific conduct) up to the most severe (full removal of parental authority)
- § 1666 (3) BGB explicitly enumerates measures available "in particular"

The doctrinal advantage: the German framework is highly flexible — once the welfare-jeopardy threshold is crossed, the court has wide discretion to fashion the remedy proportionate to the conduct. The doctrinal disadvantage: less structural transparency than the Italian three-step.

### Austrian model — Obsorge-based escalation

Austria's framework is anchored on the *Obsorge* (parental responsibility) concept:

- § 187 (2) ABGB — court must restrict or prohibit contact where the non-residential parent breaches § 159 *Wohlverhaltensgebot*
- § 177 ABGB — transfer of *Obsorge* to one parent
- § 181 ABGB — full *Entzug der Obsorge* (deprivation of parental responsibility) in the most severe cases

Austria's framework most closely resembles Italy's structurally: the three tiers (contact restriction → custody transfer → Obsorge deprivation) parallel the Italian art. 333 / art. 337-quater / art. 330 ladder.

### UK model — flexibility through Children Act 1989 s. 8 + s. 31 + s. 91A architecture

England & Wales operates a **judicial-discretion-rich** framework:

- s. 8 — child arrangements orders (the principal contact/residence vehicle)
- s. 16 — Family Assistance Order (light-touch supervision)
- s. 31 — care and supervision orders (the "significant harm" threshold)
- s. 91A — barring orders (preventing further applications by abusive litigants)

The UK framework intentionally avoids prescribed tiers in favour of judicial discretion under the welfare-paramountcy principle (s. 1) and welfare checklist (s. 1 (3)). The doctrinal disadvantage: less predictability than civil-law graduated frameworks; the advantage: the court can tailor the remedy to the specific facts without being constrained by tier-thresholds.

The introduction of s. 91A by the Domestic Abuse Act 2021 specifically addressed the abusive-litigation pattern that is a frequent feature of high-conflict alienation cases — barring orders allow the court to prevent serial applications used as harassment tools.

## Use in alienating-conduct cases — comparative outcomes

### Tier 3 (intermediate measures) thresholds

| Jurisdiction | Trigger threshold | Burden of proof |
|---|---|---|
| Italy art. 333 | *condotta pregiudizievole* (prejudicial conduct) | Welfare-tribunal evidential standard |
| Germany § 1666 | *Kindeswohlgefährdung* (jeopardy to child welfare) | Welfare-court evidential standard |
| Austria § 187 (2) | Breach of § 159 *Wohlverhaltensgebot* + welfare assessment | AußStrG non-contentious standard |
| UK s. 31 | "Significant harm" (s. 31 (2)) | Civil standard (balance of probabilities, but elevated practice) |

### Tier 5 (deprivation) outcomes

| Jurisdiction | Statutory trigger | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Italy art. 330 | *grave pregiudizio* | Deprivation of *responsabilità genitoriale* (decision-making authority); parental status retained |
| Germany § 1666 (severe) | Persistent Kindeswohlgefährdung where lesser measures inadequate | Removal of parental authority (full or partial) |
| Austria § 181 | Sustained welfare jeopardy where § 187 (2) restriction inadequate | *Entzug der Obsorge* — Obsorge transfers to other parent or to youth welfare authority |
| UK s. 31 (care order) | "Significant harm" attributable to parental care | Local authority acquires parental responsibility; child taken into care |

The UK care-order outcome is structurally different from the civil-law decadenza/Entzug — in the UK the local authority *acquires* parental responsibility (shared with parents) rather than transferring it to the other parent. In sustained-PA cases this matters: where both parents are insufficient (e.g. alienating parent's conduct + targeted parent's diminished engagement after years of frustration), the UK system can intervene third-party, while the civil-law systems typically transfer between parents.

### Tier 6 (rehabilitation) — the structural commitment to reversibility

All four jurisdictions provide explicit rehabilitation routes:

- Italy art. 332 — *reintegrazione* with both prongs (cessate ragioni + escluso pericolo)
- Germany § 1696 — *Abänderung gerichtlicher Entscheidungen* on change of circumstances
- Austria § 182 — *Wiederherstellung der Obsorge*
- UK s. 39 — *discharge of care order*

The doctrinal commitment to reversibility is uniform across all four: deprivation/restriction is not permanent, and the systems are oriented toward restoration when the underlying conditions have been resolved. This is a critical feature in alienating-conduct cases where the alienating parent may engage with therapeutic intervention and demonstrate sustained behavioural change.

## Why this comparative entry matters

- **Cross-border PA practice.** Cross-border alienation cases routinely involve corridor pairs (DE–IT, AT–IT, UK–IT, DE–UK) where practitioners need to map remedies between jurisdictions. This entry provides that map.
- **Selection of remedy.** A practitioner choosing between tier-3 and tier-5 escalation needs to understand the threshold differences. The comparative table above gives the operational thresholds across the four jurisdictions.
- **Rehabilitation framing.** Where a targeted parent (or supporting therapeutic professional) is weighing whether to pursue tier-5 remedies, the explicit rehabilitation route in each system is doctrinally important — these are not one-way doors.
