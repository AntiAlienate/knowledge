# CEE cooperation-duty + contact-framework comparative — PL, CZ, HU, RO, SI, HR

**Jurisdiction:** Comparative · **Coverage:** Poland · Czech Republic · Hungary · Romania · Slovenia · Croatia

A side-by-side analytical comparison of the six Central and Eastern European cooperation-duty + contact frameworks — the post-socialist family codes that recodified parental responsibility and contact regulation after 1990. The CEE jurisdictions converge on a *welfare-of-the-child* + *joint-exercise* + *cooperation-duty* framework, but diverge significantly on facilitation-duty articulation, enforcement mechanism, and PA-pattern responsiveness.

## Four-tier classification by facilitation-duty articulation strength

### Tier 1 — Express positive-action duty (most explicit)

**Slovenia (DZ art. 143 (2))** — *prizadevati si mora* (must strive). The Slovenian framework expressly codifies a *positive-effort* duty: the resident parent must not merely refrain from obstruction but must *actively strive* for the child's *appropriate relationship toward contact* with the other parent. This is doctrinally distinctive — directly addresses the alienation-pattern psychological dynamic (the child's relationship toward contact, not just contact itself).

**Croatia (OZ art. 117 (2))** — *dužan je omogućiti* (obliged to enable). The Croatian framework codifies a *positive-enable* duty alongside the negative-refrain duty. The dual-prong structure operates similarly to Slovenia's framework. The reciprocal duty at art. 117 (3) on the contact-exercising parent closes the structural gap.

### Tier 2 — Express dual right-and-obligation framing

**Poland (KRO art. 113 § 1)** — *prawo i obowiązek* (right and obligation). The 2009 nowelizacja's right-and-obligation framing closes the structural gap where the targeted parent could be passive in the face of alienation. The non-resident parent has both a right *and* an obligation to maintain contact — passivity is not lawful.

**Hungary (Ptk § 4:178 (2))** — *jogosult és köteles* (entitled and obliged) + *zavartalan kapcsolattartást biztosítani* (ensure undisturbed contact). The Hungarian framework combines the right-and-obligation framing with an *ensure undisturbed contact* duty on the resident parent — operationally close to the Slovenian/Croatian positive duties.

**Romania (Codul civil art. 487)** — *dreptul și îndatorirea* (right and obligation). The Romanian framing operates at the parental-authority content level (art. 487) and through the post-divorce *în comun* (joint) default (art. 397).

### Tier 3 — Express mutual-respect duty

**Czech Republic (NOZ § 891 (1))** — *respektovat se navzájem* (respect each other) + *umožnit styk* (enable contact). The Czech framework expressly codifies mutual respect alongside the enable-contact duty. The *respektovat* framing provides structural foundation for PA-pattern findings where the alienating parent's conduct demonstrates disrespect for the targeted parent's role.

## Doctrinal analysis — five key dimensions

### 1. Right + obligation framing

| Jurisdiction | Framing | Source provision |
|---|---|---|
| Poland | *prawo i obowiązek* | KRO art. 113 § 1 |
| Hungary | *jogosult és köteles* | Ptk § 4:178 (2) |
| Romania | *dreptul și îndatorirea* | Codul civil art. 487 |
| Slovenia | Implicit via art. 143 (2) duties | DZ art. 143 (2) |
| Croatia | *dužni i imaju pravo* (duty first) | OZ art. 95 (1) |
| Czech Republic | Implicit via § 865 (1) | NOZ § 865 (1) |

Poland, Hungary, Romania, and Croatia have express right + obligation framing in their statutes. Croatia is distinctive for placing *dužni* (duty) before *pravo* (right) — doctrinally prioritising parental obligation. Slovenia and Czech Republic rely on the duty-content of the framework provisions to achieve equivalent operational effect.

### 2. Resident parent's affirmative facilitation duty

| Jurisdiction | Duty articulation | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Slovenia | *prizadevati si mora* — must strive for child's appropriate relationship toward contact | Strongest — positive psychological-relational |
| Croatia | *dužan je omogućiti* — obliged to enable contact | Strong — positive operational |
| Hungary | *zavartalan kapcsolattartást biztosítani* — ensure undisturbed contact | Strong — positive operational |
| Czech Republic | *umožnit styk* — enable contact | Medium — positive operational |
| Romania | Indirect via art. 401 + § 487 | Medium — implicit |
| Poland | Indirect via art. 113⁶ enforcement | Medium — implicit |

Slovenia's *prizadevati si mora* is the doctrinally strongest CEE articulation — addressing the child's *relationship toward contact*, which directly engages the alienation-pattern psychological dynamic.

### 3. Contact-restriction threshold

| Jurisdiction | Threshold | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| Poland | KRO art. 113³ *poważnie zagraża* (seriously endangers) | High — prohibition only on serious-endangerment |
| Romania | Art. 401 (2) via *interesul superior* (welfare) | Medium |
| Hungary | Ptk § 4:181 court-fixed via *gyermek érdeke* | Medium |
| Slovenia | DZ art. 144 (2) — only on *grounds determined in this Code* + *psihična obremenitev* | High — closed list |
| Croatia | OZ art. 121 *nužno* (necessary) + *osobito* enumeration | High — necessity + enumerated grounds |
| Czech Republic | NOZ § 891 (2) *potřebné v zájmu dítěte* | Medium |

Poland (*poważnie zagraża*), Slovenia (*samo iz razlogov*), and Croatia (*nužno* + *osobito*) have the highest contact-restriction thresholds — making contact-denial doctrinally more difficult to justify.

### 4. Enforcement mechanism

| Jurisdiction | Primary enforcement | Distinctive feature |
|---|---|---|
| Poland | *odebranie dziecka* — KPC arts. 598¹-598¹⁴ | *Kurator sądowy* (court-appointed curator) supervision |
| Czech Republic | Soud opatrovnický (guardianship court) | *Mediace* under §§ 865-907 |
| Hungary | *Gyámhatóság* (guardianship authority) + civil-damages liability under § 4:181 (5) | **Express civil-damages liability for breach** |
| Romania | *Mediere* under Legea nr. 192/2006 | DGASPC psychosocial inquiry |
| Slovenia | *Center za socialno delo* mediation + *okrožno sodišče* | 2019 reform CSD → specialised family courts |
| Croatia | *Obiteljsko savjetovanje* (family counselling) | *Hrvatski zavod za socijalni rad* network |

Hungary's express *civil-damages liability* under § 4:181 (5) is doctrinally distinctive — providing a structural enforcement mechanism beyond the typical fine/coercive-measures framework. The alienating parent's contact-frustration can generate damages liability under civil-law liability rules.

### 5. Joint-exercise default

All six CEE jurisdictions establish joint exercise of parental responsibility as the structural default after separation, but the formula varies:

| Jurisdiction | Default formula | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Poland | KRO art. 97 § 1 *współnie wykonywanie* (joint exercise) | Polish framework |
| Czech Republic | NOZ § 875 *společně* + § 907 (1) three-option | Three-option care allocation |
| Hungary | Ptk § 4:167 (1) *közösen* on *lényeges kérdések* | Joint on essential matters even post-divorce |
| Romania | Codul civil art. 397 *în comun* post-divorce default | Joint default post-divorce |
| Slovenia | DZ art. 141 (1) *skupno in sporazumno* | Joint and by agreement |
| Croatia | OZ art. 95 (2) *sporazumno* | By agreement (sporazumno) |

The Hungarian and Romanian frameworks are doctrinally distinctive in expressly preserving joint exercise of *essential matters* (Hungary) or *în comun* exercise (Romania) even where one parent is the day-to-day primary carer.

## Operational implications for PA-pattern cases

### Where the framework is structurally most contact-protective

**Slovenia (DZ art. 143 (2)).** The *prizadevati si mora* positive-effort duty is the doctrinally strongest anti-alienation provision in CEE law. The resident parent's duty to *strive for the child's appropriate relationship toward contact* directly addresses the alienation-pattern psychological dynamic. The combined operation with the *opustiti vse* (refrain from all) negative duty provides comprehensive structural protection.

**Croatia (OZ art. 117 (2)).** The dual-prong *dužan je omogućiti* + *suzdržati se od svakog djelovanja* framework is operationally close to the Slovenian framework. The *svakog* (all) qualifier means even minor obstructive conduct engages the duty.

**Hungary (Ptk § 4:178 (2) + § 4:181 (5)).** The *jogosult és köteles* + *zavartalan kapcsolattartást biztosítani* framework combined with express civil-damages liability provides the strongest CEE enforcement framework — alienating-conduct breach generates damages liability under civil-law rules.

### Where additional reasoning is required

**Poland (KRO art. 113-113⁶).** The Polish framework is operationally strong (right-and-obligation framing, graduated restriction ladder, grandparents extension) but the facilitation-duty articulation is more indirect. PA-pattern argument is built through the *prawo i obowiązek* framing + the welfare standard.

**Czech Republic (NOZ §§ 865-928).** The Czech framework operates through the *respektovat se navzájem* mutual-respect duty + the *střídavá péče* Ústavní-soud-preferred residence option. The Pl. ÚS 26/2014 jurisprudence has operationalised the framework.

**Romania (Codul civil art. 397 + 401 + 487).** The Romanian framework is operationally consolidated by the 2024 amendments to arts. 483-512 (alienation-recognition language). The *în comun* default and *dreptul și îndatorirea* framing provide structural anchors.

## Cross-cutting CEE features

### 1. Capacity-based child's voice standard

All six jurisdictions use capacity-based standards, but with significant variation:

| Jurisdiction | Standard | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Poland | KRO art. 95 § 4 *stosownie do okoliczności* | Capacity-based |
| Czech Republic | NOZ § 867 + § 100 OSŘ | Capacity-based |
| Hungary | Ptk § 4:171 (3) | Age 14 for mandatory hearing |
| Romania | Codul civil art. 264 | Age 10 mandatory + reasoning-rejection |
| Slovenia | ZPP art. 410 | Capacity-based |
| Croatia | ZPP art. 360 | Capacity-based |

Romania's age-10 mandatory hearing + reasoning-rejection requirement is doctrinally distinctive — courts cannot dismiss hearing applications without express reasoning. Hungary retains the older age-14 threshold from the 1952 framework.

### 2. Welfare-paramountcy framing

| Jurisdiction | Welfare formula |
|---|---|
| Poland | *Dobro dziecka* — welfare of the child |
| Czech Republic | *Zájem dítěte* — interest of the child (with Ústavní soud doctrinal development) |
| Hungary | *Gyermek érdeke* — interest of the child |
| Romania | *Interesul superior al copilului* — superior interest of the child |
| Slovenia | *Otrokova korist* — child's interest |
| Croatia | *Dobrobit djeteta* — welfare of the child |

The Romanian *interesul superior* formula and the Czech *zájem dítěte* (with constitutional development) are doctrinally strongest.

### 3. Mediation framework

All six jurisdictions have structurally embedded mediation, but with varying institutional design:

- Poland: *Mediacja* under KRO art. 113¹ § 1
- Czech Republic: Mandatory mediation options under NOZ §§ 865-907
- Hungary: *Közvetítés* under guardianship authority
- Romania: *Mediere* under Legea nr. 192/2006
- Slovenia: *Center za socialno delo* under DZ art. 144 (1)
- Croatia: *Obiteljsko savjetovanje* under OZ arts. 326-329

### 4. Constitutional Court engagement

| Jurisdiction | Constitutional Court | PA-recognition jurisprudence |
|---|---|---|
| Poland | Trybunał Konstytucyjny | Limited engagement |
| Czech Republic | Ústavní soud | Pl. ÚS 26/2014 + Pl. ÚS 23/2008 (střídavá péče presumption) |
| Hungary | Alkotmánybíróság | Limited engagement |
| Romania | Curtea Constituțională | Decizia CCR nr. 482/2017 (în comun primacy) |
| Slovenia | Ustavno sodišče | Up-383/22 (contact-facilitation primacy) |
| Croatia | Ustavni sud | U-IIIB-1373/2009 (Article 8 reception) |

The Czech Ústavní soud's *střídavá péče* doctrine (Pl. ÚS 26/2014) is the most developed CEE constitutional jurisprudence on shared residence.
